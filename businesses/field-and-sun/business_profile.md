## 1. Identity

- **Business name:** Field & Sun
- **Website URL:** <https://fieldandsun.com>
- **One-line description:** Skincare for daily life and bright days — minimalist, ingredient-led, refillable.
- **Year founded:** 2024
- **Team size (people working on SEO/marketing):** 3 (founder, marketing lead, part-time content writer)

## 2. Business model

- **Type:** D2C e-commerce
- **Primary revenue source:** product sales (90% direct via website, 10% via Amazon)
- **Average order value (AOV):** $68
- **Gross margin %:** 62%
- **Sales cycle length:** instant for first purchase; ~30-day repeat cycle on Serum refills

## 3. Products / services

Two hero products that drive 70% of revenue, plus 6 complementary SKUs that lift AOV and lock customers into a routine.

- **Category 1: Skincare (evergreen)**
  - **Hero — Daily Glow Serum (15% Vitamin C + 4% Niacinamide + Hyaluronic Acid)** — the reorder engine. Year-round daily use. Targets dullness, uneven tone, dehydration. Refillable bottle + recyclable refill pouches.
  - Bloom Cleanser (gentle gel) — entry-point product, low ticket ($22), daily use locks routine.
  - Glow Mask (weekly exfoliating treatment) — AOV booster; bundles naturally with Serum.
  - Eye Cream (peptide + caffeine) — regimen-completer, year-round.

- **Category 2: Sun care (seasonal — spring/summer peak)**
  - **Hero — Mineral Sun Drops SPF 50** — tinted mineral (zinc oxide) sunscreen, no white cast. Peak demand April–August. Available year-round for skincare-disciplined buyers.
  - After-Sun Aloe Mist — seasonal complement, summer add-on.
  - SPF Lip Balm 30 — small-ticket impulse add-on, year-round but emphasised in summer.

- **Category 3: Bundles & refills**
  - Routine Refill Pouches (for the Daily Glow Serum) — sustainability angle, repeat-purchase flywheel, ~$12 vs $42 for a fresh bottle.
  - Routine Bundle (Cleanser + Serum + Sun Drops) — 15% off vs individual; loyalty + AOV play. ~25% of new-customer orders.

## 4. Customer

- **Primary customer description:** Women 28–45 in the US, skincare-literate, ingredient-conscious, willing to invest in 1-2 quality products over a closet of cheap ones. Reads ingredient lists. Cares about packaging waste. Distrustful of "clean beauty" marketing language and prefers specifics (percentages, clinical context).
- **Top 3 customer pains your product solves:**
  1. Most mineral sunscreens leave a white cast and feel chalky on skin.
  2. Vitamin C serums oxidize quickly, lose potency, and break the bank when replaced every few weeks.
  3. Beauty product packaging creates a lot of plastic waste, and most "refillable" options don't actually save money or work seamlessly.
- **Top 3 questions customers ask before buying:**
  1. Does the SPF leave a white cast on my skin tone? (especially asked by darker-skinned customers)
  2. How long does a bottle last and what's the per-day cost vs alternatives?
  3. Are the ingredients clinically supported, and at what percentages?
- **Geographic markets:** US (90% of revenue), Canada (8%), UK (2%)
- **Languages you operate in:** English

## 7. Goals

- **Primary SEO goal for the next 6 months:** 2x organic sessions to ~17,000/mo, with organic search delivering 30% of new-customer acquisition (currently ~18%).
- **Secondary goal:** Rank top 3 for the "tinted mineral sunscreen" cluster (primary keyword + 6-8 supporting variants) before peak season Q2 next year.
- **Constraints:**
  - 3-person team; founder splits time across product/ops/marketing.
  - Content production budget: ~$2k/mo (1 writer at 4 long-form posts/mo + occasional product copy).
  - No dedicated dev resources beyond Shopify theme tweaks (any structural changes require an agency or contract dev).
  - Q2/Q3 demand spike on Sun Drops creates inventory + content pressure simultaneously.

## 8. Brand voice / editorial guardrails

- **Tone:** warm, knowledgeable, plain-language. Confident without being preachy. Uses ingredient detail without being clinical or jargon-heavy.
- **Words / phrases to avoid:**
  - "clean beauty" / "clean" (overused; we lean on ingredient specifics instead)
  - "non-toxic" / "chemical-free" (scientifically wrong; reads as misinformed)
  - "the ultimate," "miracle," "transformative"
  - "anti-aging" (we say "supports skin over time" or specifics)
  - Excessive emoji or exclamation points
- **Words / phrases that are on-brand:**
  - "Ingredient-led" / "what's in it and why it matters"
  - "Skincare for everyday wear" / "everyday-skin SPF"
  - Specific percentages and clinical context ("15% L-ascorbic acid," "20% non-nano zinc oxide")
  - "Refillable" (we earned it — pouches really do refill the bottle)
- **Style references** (URLs of pages whose tone we'd like to emulate):
  - <https://www.versedskin.com/blogs/all> (educational, plain-language, ingredient-led)
  - <https://saiehello.com/products> (clean copy, no overclaims)
  - <https://theordinary.com> (no-nonsense ingredient labelling — too clinical for us, but the discipline is the reference)
