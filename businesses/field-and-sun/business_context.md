## 3. Business Model

- **Revenue model:** Product sales, mostly direct-to-consumer.

## 4. Products, Services, and Capabilities

### Category 1: Skincare (evergreen)

- **Hero — Daily Glow Serum (15% Vitamin C + 4% Niacinamide + Hyaluronic Acid):** The reorder engine. Year-round daily use. Targets dullness, uneven tone, and dehydration. Refillable bottle plus recyclable refill pouches.
- **Bloom Cleanser:** Gentle gel cleanser; entry-point product, low ticket ($22), daily use locks routine.
- **Glow Mask:** Weekly exfoliating treatment; AOV booster that bundles naturally with Serum.
- **Eye Cream:** Peptide + caffeine regimen-completer, year-round.

### Category 2: Sun care (seasonal — spring/summer peak)

- **Hero — Mineral Sun Drops SPF 50:** Tinted mineral zinc oxide sunscreen with no white cast. Peak demand April-August. Available year-round for skincare-disciplined buyers.
- **After-Sun Aloe Mist:** Seasonal complement, summer add-on.
- **SPF Lip Balm 30:** Small-ticket impulse add-on, year-round but emphasised in summer.

### Category 3: Bundles and refills

- **Routine Refill Pouches:** Refills for Daily Glow Serum; sustainability angle, repeat-purchase flywheel, approximately $12 vs $42 for a fresh bottle.
- **Routine Bundle:** Cleanser + Serum + Sun Drops; 15% off vs individual, loyalty + AOV play.

## 5. Customers and Buyers

- **Primary customer description:** Women 28-45 in the US, skincare-literate, ingredient-conscious, willing to invest in 1-2 quality products over a closet of cheap ones. Reads ingredient lists. Cares about packaging waste. Distrustful of vague category language and prefers specifics such as percentages and clinical context.
- **Primary buyers / decision-makers:** The end customer buys direct; no B2B buying committee.
- **Customer pains in their own language:**
  1. Most mineral sunscreens leave a white cast and feel chalky on skin.
  2. Vitamin C serums oxidize quickly, lose potency, and break the bank when replaced every few weeks.
  3. Beauty product packaging creates a lot of plastic waste, and most refillable options do not actually save money or work seamlessly.
- **Questions customers ask before buying:**
  1. Does the SPF leave a white cast on my skin tone, especially for darker skin?
  2. How long does a bottle last and what is the per-day cost vs alternatives?
  3. Are the ingredients clinically supported, and at what percentages?
- **Red flags / bad-fit patterns:** Customers looking for miracle claims, inflated transformation promises, or unsupported safety claims are not the right fit.
- **Geographic markets:** US (90% of revenue), Canada (8%), UK (2%).
- **Languages you operate in:** English.

## 6. Positioning and Messaging

- **Category framing:** Ingredient-led daily skincare and everyday-skin SPF with refillable routines.
- **Positioning note:** Field & Sun should be specific, practical, and evidence-aware. It should not lean on vague category language or fear-based safety claims.
- **Audience promise / canonical pitch lines:** Skincare for daily life and bright days; what is in it and why it matters; everyday-skin SPF.
- **Core differentiators:** Ingredient specificity, no-white-cast mineral SPF, refillable serum routine, plain-language education, and a tight routine rather than an overwhelming SKU catalog.
- **Claims that need evidence or care:** SPF, ingredient percentages, clinical support, skin-tone suitability, refill economics, and packaging-waste claims.
- **Terms or framings to avoid:** "clean beauty," "clean," "non-toxic," "chemical-free," "the ultimate," "miracle," "transformative," "anti-aging," excessive emoji, and excessive exclamation points.

## 7. Competitors and Alternatives

- **Direct competitors:**
  - Supergoop — <https://supergoop.com> (mineral SPF leader, broad SKU range)
  - Saie Beauty — <https://saiehello.com> (mineral SPF + complexion overlap)
  - ILIA Beauty — <https://iliabeauty.com> (tinted SPF, similar positioning)
- **Indirect alternatives:** Customers may choose drugstore SPF, dermatologist-recommended pharmacy brands, or single-ingredient skincare brands instead of Field & Sun.
- **Aspirational or reference companies:** Glossier — <https://glossier.com> (D2C beauty leader, brand-led, larger scale; useful for mature-brand patterns even though not direct SKU overlap).
- **Win/loss themes:** Field & Sun wins with buyers who want specific ingredient context, refillable routines that are practical, and mineral SPF that feels wearable. It loses when buyers want the cheapest SPF, maximalist routines, or broad trend-led beauty claims.

## 8. Brand Voice and Editorial Guardrails

- **Tone:** Warm, knowledgeable, plain-language. Confident without being preachy. Uses ingredient detail without being clinical or jargon-heavy.
- **Voice principles:** Prefer specifics over broad claims. Explain what is in the formula and why it matters. Keep routines simple and practical.
- **Words / phrases to avoid:**
  - "clean beauty" / "clean"
  - "non-toxic" / "chemical-free"
  - "the ultimate," "miracle," "transformative"
  - "anti-aging" (use "supports skin over time" or specific benefit language)
  - Excessive emoji or exclamation points
- **Words / phrases that are on-brand:**
  - "Ingredient-led" / "what's in it and why it matters"
  - "Skincare for everyday wear" / "everyday-skin SPF"
  - Specific percentages and clinical context ("15% L-ascorbic acid," "20% non-nano zinc oxide")
  - "Refillable" when tied to the earned refill pouch system
- **Style references:**
  - <https://www.versedskin.com/blogs/all> (educational, plain-language, ingredient-led)
  - <https://saiehello.com/products> (clean copy, no overclaims)
  - <https://theordinary.com> (disciplined ingredient labelling; too clinical for Field & Sun, but useful as a reference)
