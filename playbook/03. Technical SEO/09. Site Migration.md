# Site Migration

> Re-platforms, rebrands, domain moves, mass URL restructures. The single highest-stakes event in technical SEO — most teams need it once every 3-5 years and most teams lose 20-40% of organic traffic when they botch it.

## What it is

A site migration is any change that shifts a meaningful share of your URLs to new locations or your site to a new platform. Common types:

- **Re-platforming** — moving from one CMS to another (WooCommerce → Shopify, Shopify → Hydrogen, custom → headless).
- **Rebranding** — changing domains (`oldbrand.com` → `newbrand.com`).
- **Mass URL restructure** — keeping the platform but reorganising URL conventions across hundreds or thousands of URLs.
- **Sub-domain consolidation** — merging `blog.example.com` into `example.com/blog`, or vice versa.
- **HTTP → HTTPS** — historical, but still relevant for legacy sites.

Migrations are high-stakes because they touch every layer of technical SEO simultaneously. Crawl, indexing, canonicals, sitemaps, internal links, redirects — all change at once. A mistake in any one of them costs ranking, sometimes permanently. A successful migration is invisible: traffic dips for 1-2 weeks during recovery, then returns to baseline (or above, if migration was an opportunity to consolidate cleanly).

For Field & Sun on Shopify, migration becomes relevant if the brand re-platforms (e.g. moves to Hydrogen for headless), expands internationally with country sub-domains, or changes its core domain. Most other on-page and technical SEO work is forward-going hygiene; migration is event-driven and high-pressure.

## Why it matters

The math is simple and unforgiving: a URL change without a 301 redirect immediately drops the page's ranking. With a clean 301, the page typically recovers 90%+ of its ranking within 30-90 days. Across hundreds of URLs, the difference between a planned migration and a botched one is the difference between a 5-15% temporary dip versus a 30-60% permanent loss.

Beyond rankings, migration is the technical change most likely to interact badly with downstream systems: analytics tracking breaks, conversion pixels stop firing on the new URLs, paid-search landing pages return 404, email-marketing links go dead. The SEO redirect map is necessary but not sufficient — every consumer of the URL inventory needs an updated map.

## Core concepts

- **301 vs 302.** 301 = permanent (passes ranking signal); 302 = temporary (does not). Migrations always use 301. Period.
- **Redirect map.** The complete list of `old URL → new URL → 301`. Every old URL that earned ranking must have a row. Building this map is the central artifact of any migration.
- **Chain redirects.** `A → B → C → D` is a chain. Each hop dilutes signal slightly and slows crawl. Always collapse: `A → D` directly. Multi-hop chains are the single most common migration mistake.
- **Internal-link sweep.** After URL changes, every internal link sitewide should point at the new URL — not at the old URL relying on the redirect to forward it. Hop redirects from internal links are wasteful; broken internal links are worse.
- **Sitemap update.** New sitemap with new URLs. Old URLs out. Submit to GSC immediately.
- **Pre-migration baseline.** Before migration, capture: full URL inventory, traffic / clicks / impressions per URL (last 90 days), ranking positions for top 50-200 keywords, GSC indexing status. Without baseline, you can't measure recovery.
- **Post-migration monitoring window.** First 7 days: high-frequency monitoring (daily GSC checks, log analysis, rank tracker). 8-30 days: standard monitoring + investigation of any URL that hasn't recovered. 30-90 days: long-tail recovery. Don't declare migration complete before 90 days.
- **Soft launch / staged rollout.** For large sites, rolling out URL-by-URL or section-by-section reduces risk. Most teams ship at-once and accept the higher dip; staged is a hedge.
- **Downstream system updates.** Analytics, paid-search landing pages, email links, app deep links, partner integrations, embedded widgets — every consumer of the URL inventory needs the new map. SEO redirect map alone isn't enough.
- **Rollback plan.** What happens if traffic doesn't recover within 30 days? Identify the specific symptoms (URL-level) and define what triggers a rollback decision. Most teams don't define this and end up paralysed when something goes wrong.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun decides to move its blog from `/blogs/news/<slug>` (Shopify default) to `/blog/<slug>` (per their URL conventions). 25 blog posts. The blog drives ~30% of organic traffic.
>
> **Step 1.** Pre-migration baseline. Pull GSC data for the 25 blog URLs over the last 90 days. Document: total impressions / clicks / position per URL, top 50 ranking keywords across all 25 URLs. Snapshot internal-link inventory pointing at each blog URL. Snapshot the current sitemap.
>
> **Step 2.** Build the redirect map. 25 rows. Each row: `/blogs/news/<slug>` → `/blog/<slug>` (301). Validate every new URL returns 200 in staging before shipping.
>
> **Step 3.** Internal-link sweep prep. Identify every internal link to old blog URLs from: navigation, footer, breadcrumbs, related-content modules, in-body contextual links across PDPs, collections, other blog posts. ~80 internal links to update.
>
> **Step 4.** Ship. Update Shopify blog handle (the one CMS change that flips all 25 URLs at once). Push redirects via Shopify URL Redirects (one per blog post). Update internal links sitewide. Re-publish sitemap with new URLs. Submit new sitemap to GSC.
>
> **Step 5.** Monitor for 90 days. Day 1-7: daily GSC checks for indexing errors, daily rank-tracker checks for the top 25 keywords. Most posts recover position within 14 days. By day 30: 23 of 25 posts back at or near baseline; 2 long-tail posts still recovering. Day 60: all 25 recovered. Day 90: no degradation; migration complete.
>
> **Step 6.** Document. Add a decisions-log entry to `businesses/field-and-sun/CLAUDE.md` noting the migration date, scope, baseline → recovery delta, and any lessons.

## How to do it

1. **Plan before touching anything.** Use the `plan-site-migration` skill to scope: which URLs change, what the new URLs are, what the redirect strategy is, what the QA gates are, what the recovery monitoring looks like, what the rollback trigger is.
2. **Capture pre-migration baseline.** GSC export of all URLs with impressions/clicks/position. Rank-tracker snapshot of top keywords. Sitemap snapshot. Internal-link inventory if possible. Without baseline, you can't measure recovery.
3. **Build the redirect map.** Use the `build-redirect-map` skill. Every old URL that earned ranking must have a row. Validate every new URL returns 200 (in staging if possible). No chains; if a URL was already redirected, redirect it to the final destination, not the intermediate.
4. **Pre-flight checks in staging if available.** New URLs return 200 / canonical / indexable. New sitemap is valid. Robots.txt allows the new URLs. Schema markup on the new pages validates in Rich Results Test.
5. **Update downstream systems before launch.** GA4 tracking, Klaviyo email links, paid-search landing pages, partner integrations, embedded widgets. SEO redirects alone don't fix these.
6. **Ship the migration.** Time of day matters less than people think for SEO; matters more for support load and rollback agility. Ship when the team is available to monitor.
7. **Submit new sitemap to GSC immediately.** Don't wait for Google to discover new URLs via crawl.
8. **High-frequency monitoring days 1-7.** Daily: GSC URL inspection on top 10 priority URLs, GSC Pages report for new errors, rank-tracker on top 50 keywords, server logs for 4xx/5xx spikes from Googlebot.
9. **Standard monitoring days 8-30.** Same checks at lower frequency. Investigate any URL that hasn't recovered ranking within 14 days — usually a redirect issue or an internal-link issue.
10. **Long-tail monitoring days 30-90.** Long-tail keywords recover slower. Don't declare migration complete before 90 days.
11. **Document.** Update business CLAUDE.md with the migration entry: date, scope, baseline → recovery delta, lessons learned. This is institutional knowledge for the next migration.

## Common pitfalls

- **Botching 301s as 302s.** The single most common mistake. 302 doesn't pass ranking. Always 301 for migrations.
- **Multi-hop redirect chains.** Old URL → middle URL → new URL. Collapse to a single hop.
- **Missing redirects on long-tail URLs.** Teams catch the top 50 URLs and miss the next 500. Long-tail traffic erosion is the slow drip that adds up to 20%+ permanent loss.
- **No internal-link sweep.** Internal links still pointing at old URLs send Googlebot through a redirect on every internal navigation. Wastes crawl budget and signals "we forgot."
- **Sitemap not updated.** Old URLs in the sitemap submit a contradictory signal — "these are our canonical URLs" — even though they're now redirects. Update and re-submit.
- **No baseline.** Without pre-migration data, you can't measure recovery. The "we lost 30% of traffic" claim is unfalsifiable without baseline.
- **Migration during peak season.** Don't migrate in November (D2C peak), April-May (Field & Sun's sun-care peak season), or any other revenue-critical window. Off-peak only.
- **No rollback plan.** When something goes wrong, the team paralyses because no one defined "what triggers rollback." Define it before launch.
- **Forgetting downstream systems.** Analytics breaks, paid-search lands on 404s, email links die. SEO redirects don't fix these. Coordinate with the wider team.
- **Declaring victory at 30 days.** Long-tail keywords recover slower. Wait 90 days.

## Skills in this toolkit

- **[plan-site-migration](../../skills/technical/plan-site-migration/SKILL.md)** — produces a written migration plan: scope (which URLs change, what type of migration), redirect strategy, pre-migration baseline checklist, sequencing (single-shot vs staged), QA gates, recovery monitoring schedule, rollback trigger definition, downstream-system update list, and post-migration documentation requirements. Run this first; the plan is the prerequisite to any other migration work.
- **[build-redirect-map](../../skills/technical/build-redirect-map/SKILL.md)** — builds the operational old-URL → new-URL → 301 mapping. Inputs: full URL inventory (sitemap + crawler), the new URL convention or per-URL mapping rules, any existing redirect file. Outputs: the mapping table with chain-detection (no multi-hop), 200-validation status per new URL, batch order for staged rollout, and an implementation-ready file for the CMS or server.
