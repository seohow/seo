# NLP Optimization

> Aligning your content with how search NLP models and LLMs parse meaning — entity-rich writing, clean topical signalling, knowledge-graph alignment. The signal layer of AI SEO, sitting between the prose-level patterns and the strategic citation layer.

## What it is

Natural Language Processing (NLP) optimisation is the practice of making your content unambiguous to the systems — Google's ranking models, Bing's, Perplexity's retrieval layer, and the LLMs behind ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini — that parse it for meaning. Every modern search and AI system represents content as a web of *entities* (named things — ingredients, regulators, products, places, concepts) and the *relationships* between them. The clearer those entities and relationships are on the page, the more reliably the system understands what the page is about, what it isn't about, and where it sits in the broader topical map.

NLP optimisation overlaps with traditional SEO concepts (topical authority, semantic search, entity SEO) but the AI-search era has raised the stakes. LLMs reason over entity relationships when synthesising answers; ambiguous or thin entity signals make the content invisible to the synthesis even if the prose is clean. A page about "vitamin C serum for dark spots" that names *L-ascorbic acid* exactly once in passing — without specifying concentration, mechanism, study sources, or related ingredients — under-performs a page that names the entity early, often, and with proximate context.

For Field & Sun, this matters because ingredient-led skincare *is* an entity-rich domain. Specific molecules (L-ascorbic acid, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, non-nano zinc oxide), concentrations, mechanisms, regulatory bodies (FDA, EMA), study sources, and competitor products are all named entities. A brand that signals these entities clearly earns topical authority and AI-citation eligibility; a brand that gestures at "vitamin C" without specifics gets out-cited by competitors who do the work.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **entity recognition is the foundation of AI extraction**. When ChatGPT or AI Overviews synthesise an answer, they reason over entities and relationships, not over keyword density. Pages with clear entity signalling are easier to extract from accurately, and the AI system's confidence in the extraction is higher.

Second, **topical authority is increasingly entity-graph driven**. Google has moved beyond keyword-matching to semantic-understanding-of-the-topic. A site that covers an entity (e.g. "mineral sunscreen") with 8 supporting pages that cleanly cross-reference each other, name the related entities (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide, broad-spectrum SPF, FDA Monograph), and link to authoritative sources earns topical authority that a site with 1 page on the same topic doesn't.

Third, **knowledge-graph eligibility is a citation accelerant**. When a brand or product enters Google's Knowledge Graph (visible as a Knowledge Panel for branded queries; embedded in entity-rich AI Overviews), citation rate and brand-mention quality improve. Entity-graph alignment — Wikipedia / Wikidata mentions, structured `sameAs` schema, consistent entity descriptions across the web — is how brands earn that eligibility.

## Core concepts

- **Entity vs keyword.** A keyword is a string ("vitamin c serum"). An entity is the underlying thing the string refers to (the molecule L-ascorbic acid, formulated as a topical skincare product). NLP systems care about entities; keyword density is mostly a legacy concept.
- **Salience.** Within a page, an entity has a salience score — how central it is to the page's topic. Tools like Google's Natural Language API or InLinks expose this. High-salience entities should match the page's intent; low-salience entities can be supporting context, not the focus.
- **Entity proximity and density.** How often the entity appears, how close it sits to its claims, and how it's varied across mentions (full name, common abbreviation, related variant). Three carefully-placed mentions beat fifteen scattered ones.
- **Topical authority and entity coverage.** The breadth and depth of related entities a site covers across multiple pages. A skincare site that has pages on L-ascorbic acid, niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, retinoids, AHAs, BHAs, and zinc oxide signals broader authority than a site with one vitamin-C page.
- **Knowledge graph and Wikidata.** Public, structured entity databases search engines reference. Brand and product Wikidata entries, with `sameAs` URLs back to the brand site, are a small but durable signal.
- **Schema as entity signal.** `Product`, `Article`, `FAQPage`, `Organization`, `Person` schema with explicit entity properties (e.g. `Product.brand`, `Article.about`, `Organization.sameAs`) tells search and AI systems what the page is about in machine-readable form. Schema and prose must match — schema theatre is detectable.
- **Internal-linking as relationship signal.** Links from a hub page to spoke pages with descriptive anchor text reinforce the hub-spoke topical relationship. Anchor text is read as the entity / topic the destination is about.
- **External entity references.** Linking to authoritative sources for entities (Wikipedia for an ingredient, FDA for a regulatory term, peer-reviewed study for a claim) anchors your page to the wider entity graph.
- **Entity disambiguation.** When a term is ambiguous (e.g. "Apple" the company vs the fruit; "Glow" the brand vs the cosmetic effect), use surrounding context, schema `sameAs`, and linked entities to disambiguate. AI systems and humans both need the cue.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun has a vitamin-C cluster — 1 pillar page (`/blog/vitamin-c-serum-guide`) and 4 spoke pages (one on dark spots, one on sensitive skin, one on layering with retinol, one comparing concentrations). Currently the pillar ranks position 11 for the head term ("vitamin c serum"); spokes rank in the 20s for their long-tail variants. The brand is rarely cited in AI Overviews or Perplexity for vitamin-C queries.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `audit-entity-optimisation` across the cluster. Findings:
>
> - **Pillar page entity coverage thin.** "L-ascorbic acid" mentioned 3 times; "ascorbic acid" 1 time; no mention of THD ascorbate or magnesium ascorbyl phosphate (alternative vitamin-C forms competitors discuss extensively). No mention of pH stability or oxidation, which are central to the topic.
> - **Salience misaligned.** The page's highest-salience entity is "Field & Sun" (brand) rather than "vitamin C" or "L-ascorbic acid." Brand-storytelling content is dominating signal.
> - **Internal linking sparse.** Pillar links to only 2 of 4 spokes; spokes don't link back to pillar with descriptive anchors.
> - **No schema.** No `Article` schema on the pillar; no `Product` schema linking to the Daily Glow Serum PDP; no `sameAs` on the brand `Organization` schema.
> - **No external entity references.** No links to FDA, peer-reviewed studies, or established medical / dermatology sources on factual claims.
> - **Knowledge-graph status.** Field & Sun has no Wikidata entry; brand search shows no Knowledge Panel. Daily Glow Serum has no entity recognition.
>
> **Step 2.** Remediation plan:
>
> - **Pillar rewrite** to bring up entity coverage: add sections on alternative vitamin-C forms (THD, MAP, sodium ascorbyl phosphate), pH stability, oxidation, formulation considerations. Each new section names the entity and its relationship to the topic explicitly.
> - **Salience rebalance:** move brand-storytelling to a closing section; lead with entity-rich content.
> - **Internal-linking pass:** pillar links to all 4 spokes with descriptive anchors ("vitamin C serum for dark spots," "vitamin C serum for sensitive skin"); each spoke links back to the pillar with anchor "vitamin C serum guide."
> - **Schema additions:** `Article` schema on pillar with `about` property naming the entity; `Product` schema on PDP cross-referenced from pillar via `mentions`; `Organization` schema with `sameAs` listing brand social profiles.
> - **External entity references:** add citations to FDA OTC monograph, two peer-reviewed studies on L-ascorbic acid efficacy, the EWG entry, and authoritative dermatology references.
> - **Knowledge-graph foundation:** create a Wikidata entry for the brand (Field & Sun, D2C skincare, founded 2024) and one for the Daily Glow Serum (specific product, ingredient list, brand link). Cross-link via `sameAs`.
>
> **Step 3.** Result at 90 days: pillar page entity-salience for "vitamin C serum" / "L-ascorbic acid" rises from rank-3 to rank-1 entity (per InLinks / NLP API). Pillar ranking 11 → 6. Spokes lift 20s → mid-teens. AI Overview citation gained on 2 of the 7 priority queries; Perplexity citation gained on the head query. Brand-mention quality in `audit-llm-visibility` improves from "named without context" to "named with product specificity" in 60% of mentions.

## How to do it

1. **Audit before you optimise.** Run `audit-entity-optimisation` per cluster (not per page in isolation — entity signals work cluster-wide). Identify entity-coverage gaps, salience misalignment, schema gaps, internal-link gaps, knowledge-graph status.
2. **Map the target entities per cluster.** For each cluster, list the entities the SERP top 10 cover. These are the entities your cluster needs to cover too. Tools: InLinks, MarketMuse, Surfer, or manual SERP analysis.
3. **Rebalance salience.** For each priority page, the highest-salience entity should match the page's intent. If brand-storytelling dominates a "what is vitamin C serum" page, the page is signalling the wrong topic.
4. **Densify entity coverage where thin.** Add sections, examples, comparisons that name related entities. Be specific (full chemical name, regulatory body, named study) rather than gestural ("studies show").
5. **Schema audit + fix.** `Article` schema on long-form content with `about` and `mentions`; `Product` schema on PDPs; `Organization` schema with `sameAs`; `FAQPage` only where the page genuinely has Q&A content. No schema theatre.
6. **Internal-linking pass.** Hubs link to spokes with descriptive anchors; spokes link back to hubs; related spokes cross-link where the entity relationship is clear. Avoid "click here" / "read more" anchors.
7. **External entity references.** Authoritative sources (regulators, peer-reviewed research, established publications) for factual claims. Wikipedia for entity disambiguation where the page references a generally-understood term.
8. **Knowledge-graph foundation.** Wikidata entry for the brand. Wikidata entries for hero products. `sameAs` schema linking to social profiles, Wikidata, Wikipedia (where applicable).
9. **Re-measure.** 60-90 days post-remediation, re-run the audit and `audit-llm-visibility` to confirm improved entity signals translate to AI citation and ranking lift.
10. **Bake into editorial.** Update the content brief template (Content SEO leaf 08) to require: target entity list, related entities to name, internal-link plan, external citation requirements, schema spec.

## Common pitfalls

- **Keyword-stuffing rebranded as "entity optimisation."** The point isn't to repeat the entity 50 times — it's to name it clearly, with proximate context, and varied phrasing. Spam patterns hurt.
- **Schema theatre.** Adding `FAQPage` schema with manufactured Q&As to game AI Overview eligibility. Schema must match honest on-page content. Mismatch is detectable and increasingly penalised.
- **Treating internal links as the whole job.** Internal linking is one signal. Without entity-rich prose and external authority signals, more internal links don't fix thin content.
- **Wikidata theatre.** Creating Wikidata entries for products that don't actually exist as recognised entities outside the brand's own marketing. Wikidata moderators reject low-notability entries; aggressive submissions can damage brand standing.
- **Optimising for one search engine's NLP model.** Google, Bing, and the LLMs behind major AI search products have overlapping but distinct entity-recognition behaviour. Patterns that win across all (clear naming, proximate context, schema, external authority) beat patterns optimised for one.
- **Ignoring the prose layer.** Entity-rich content can still read badly if the underlying prose is unclear or fluffy. NLP optimisation works *with* Content Optimization, not as a substitute.
- **Spammy "entity grids."** Auto-generated tables of entities with thin content per cell. Looks like topical authority; reads as scraped. AI systems and humans both downrank.
- **Forgetting the cluster context.** Optimising one page's entity coverage in isolation, while leaving the cluster's other pages untouched, signals topical inconsistency. Operate cluster-wide.
- **No measurement.** Entity audits are easy to run; impact is harder to attribute. Pair with `audit-llm-visibility` for a feedback loop.
