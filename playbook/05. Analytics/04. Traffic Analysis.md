# Traffic Analysis

> Turning raw traffic data into pattern recognition — what's growing, what's decaying, what changed when, which channels matter, where the funnel breaks. The analysis discipline that converts data into decisions. Without it, dashboards exist but nobody acts on them.

## What it is

Traffic analysis is the practice of taking GA4 (and adjacent) traffic data and finding the patterns that matter — beyond the surface-level "sessions are up 12% YoY." It looks for: which channels are growing or decaying, which pages are gaining or losing, which segments behave differently, where the funnel breaks, what changed when, and what to do about it. Traffic analysis sits between *data collection* (GA4 + GSC + rank tracking) and *KPI tracking* (the regular dashboard discipline). It's the analytical layer that produces insight, not just metrics.

For Field & Sun, traffic analysis is where the marketing team finds out: organic search drove 38% of revenue last quarter (vs 31% prior), but most of the growth came from sun-care cluster pages — not the recently-launched paid-media campaigns. The cluster pillar is producing 4x the conversion rate of the average blog post. PDP traffic from organic is flat YoY despite blog traffic growing 40%, suggesting the blog ↔ PDP linking is leaking. Each finding is a decision input that pure dashboards don't surface.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **dashboards report metrics; analysis produces decisions**. A dashboard showing "organic sessions: 84,000 (+18% YoY)" doesn't tell you what to do. Analysis breaking that down by cluster, channel, page-type, and conversion contribution does. The difference is the difference between data exhibitions and operating decisions.

Second, **traffic-pattern recognition catches problems early**. Decay shows up as patterns long before it shows up in revenue. Refresh candidates, broken funnels, mis-routed traffic, attribution leaks — all are visible in traffic patterns 4-12 weeks before they become P&L issues. Teams that look win the early-warning advantage.

Third, **traffic analysis is how SEO connects to revenue credibly**. Without analysis, "SEO is growing" is a claim. With analysis, "SEO contributed $X in attributed revenue from clusters Y and Z, growing N% QoQ" is a measurable contribution. The latter survives executive scrutiny; the former doesn't.

For Field & Sun: a quarterly traffic analysis is plausibly the highest-ROI marketing-team activity that doesn't directly produce content.

## Core concepts

- **Channels.** Organic search, direct, referral, organic social, paid social, paid search, email, and other custom buckets. GA4's default channel grouping is reasonable; some teams customise.
- **Page types.** Blog, PDP, collection, landing page, tool, programmatic — different page types convert and behave differently. Aggregating across all pages obscures the pattern.
- **Segmentation.** New vs returning users; mobile vs desktop; geo (country / region); audience (defined in GA4); landing-page cluster. Different segments tell different stories.
- **Comparison windows.** WoW (week-over-week, noisy), MoM (month-over-month, useful for seasonal patterns), QoQ (quarter-over-quarter, baseline for SEO trend), YoY (year-over-year, removes seasonality where it exists). For SEO specifically, QoQ + YoY is the right pair.
- **Attribution.** How conversion credit is distributed across touchpoints. GA4 default is data-driven attribution (DDA); last-click is also common. The choice materially changes channel-level revenue numbers.
- **Funnel analysis.** Sessions → engaged sessions → conversions. Drop-off at each stage is diagnostic.
- **Anomaly detection.** Identifying meaningful deviations from baseline (vs. noise). GA4 has built-in anomaly detection; Looker Studio + ML tooling can do more.
- **Top-N reports vs distribution analysis.** "Top 10 pages by sessions" is useful but ignores the long tail. "Sessions distribution" (curve of pages by session count) catches structural patterns top-N reports miss.
- **Cluster-level rollup.** Pages aggregate to clusters; cluster-level analysis is where strategic insight lives.
- **The "what changed" question.** When a metric shifts, the discipline is: what changed in the period? Site changes, tracking changes, algorithm updates, marketing campaigns, seasonality, external events. Trace causation, don't just observe correlation.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun's marketing team produces a monthly metrics report ("organic sessions, conversion rate, revenue") but stakeholders aren't acting on it. The team wants to upgrade the report from metrics-recap to insight-driven analysis.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `analyse-traffic-patterns`. Skill produces a structured QoQ analysis covering channel mix, page-type rollup, cluster-level insights, segment analysis, anomaly detection, and prioritised actions.
>
> **Step 2.** Findings:
>
> - **Channel mix:** organic search +24% QoQ vs paid search +6%. Organic now 38% of revenue (was 31%). Direct grew with organic — likely halo effect.
> - **Page-type rollup:** blog traffic +40%, but PDP organic traffic flat. Blog → PDP click-through rate dropped from 8.2% to 5.1%. The new sun-care pillar isn't directing traffic into product pages — internal-link audit needed.
> - **Cluster rollup:** sun-care cluster +47% sessions, +38% conversions; refills cluster -22% sessions; vitamin-C flat.
> - **Mobile vs desktop:** mobile sessions +28% but mobile conversion rate dropped from 1.8% to 1.4%. Desktop CR steady. Mobile checkout funnel investigation.
> - **New vs returning:** new users drove 71% of organic growth — SEO is acquiring new audience, not just re-engaging existing.
> - **Anomaly:** sudden drop in organic sessions on April 12-14; correlates with a Google algorithm update (per public reports). Mostly recovered by April 20.
> - **Attribution:** DDA-credited organic revenue was $124k vs last-click $96k. The team had been reporting last-click; switching to DDA tells a different story.
>
> **Step 3.** Prioritised actions:
>
> 1. Run `audit-page-internal-links` on the sun-care pillar and top blog posts; the pillar → PDP linking is the leak.
> 2. Investigate mobile checkout drop-off (handoff to UX / CRO).
> 3. Run `plan-content-refresh` for refills cluster (-22% is a meaningful decay).
> 4. Standardise on DDA attribution for monthly reports; document the change.
> 5. Schedule quarterly traffic analysis on the calendar.
>
> **Step 4.** Result: monthly metrics report becomes the quarterly traffic-analysis report; stakeholders engage; the surfaced internal-link leak is fixed within 4 weeks; PDP organic traffic recovers 22% in the following month.

## How to do it

1. **Set the comparison window.** QoQ as the default; YoY in addition where data retention allows.
2. **Pull the data.** GA4 (sessions, conversions, revenue, users), GSC (organic clicks, impressions, position), rank tracking if in scope, conversion data from source-of-truth (Shopify / CRM).
3. **Channel mix analysis.** Sessions / conversions / revenue by channel. Identify growth, decay, mix shifts.
4. **Page-type rollup.** Blog / PDP / collection / landing / tool / programmatic. Same metrics by page type.
5. **Cluster-level rollup.** Map pages to clusters from cluster map; aggregate. The strategic-insight layer.
6. **Segmentation.** Mobile vs desktop, new vs returning, geo if multi-market, audiences.
7. **Funnel analysis.** Session → engaged session → product view → ATC → checkout → purchase. Identify drop-offs.
8. **Anomaly detection.** GA4's built-in detector + manual scan for >20% MoM swings on important segments.
9. **"What changed" investigation.** For every meaningful pattern shift, ask: site changes, tracking changes, algorithm updates, campaigns, seasonality, external events.
10. **Insights & actions.** 5-10 prioritised actions, each tied to a toolkit skill or external handoff.
11. **Run quarterly.** The cadence matters. Annual is too sparse; monthly is too noisy.

## Common pitfalls

- **Reporting metrics, not insights.** "Sessions are up" isn't an insight. "Sessions are up because of cluster X, driven by new audience, with conversion-rate weakness on mobile" is.
- **Comparing 28-day windows.** Too noisy. QoQ + YoY is the SEO standard.
- **Ignoring attribution-model effects.** Last-click and DDA tell different stories. Pick one for primary reporting; document.
- **Skipping segmentation.** Aggregate metrics hide the patterns. Mobile vs desktop, new vs returning, cluster-level rollup are non-negotiable.
- **Top-10 reports without distribution context.** "Top 10 pages drove 60% of traffic" is also "the long tail drove 40%." Both halves matter.
- **Correlating without investigating causation.** Just because sessions rose when a campaign launched doesn't mean the campaign drove it. "What changed in the period" is the discipline.
- **Quarterly reports nobody reads.** Stakeholder relevance matters. Lead with revenue / conversion-rate / cluster-level insights, not session counts.
- **Skipping the actions section.** Analysis without action is reporting. Every traffic analysis should produce 5-10 specific actions.
- **Not closing the loop.** Last quarter's actions either shipped or didn't. Next quarter's analysis should reference what happened.
