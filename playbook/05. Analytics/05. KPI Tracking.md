# KPI Tracking

> Pick the right metrics, build the dashboard, set the cadence. The discipline of *what to look at, when, and what to do about it*. Without it, analytics produces data exhibitions; with it, analytics produces decisions.

## What it is

KPI tracking is the practice of choosing the right SEO key performance indicators for a business, building dashboards that surface them, and establishing the operating cadence around them (weekly / monthly / quarterly). It's the layer that connects raw data (GA4, GSC, rank tracking) to operating decisions. The right KPI set is small, business-aligned, and reviewed on a schedule; the wrong KPI set is large, vanity-aligned, and reviewed when stakeholders ask for it.

For Field & Sun, KPI tracking is what answers "are we on track this quarter?" — for the marketing team, the leadership team, and the board. The KPIs distil 50+ available metrics down to 8-12 that actually matter, surface them on dashboards stakeholders trust, and create the rhythm of weekly check-ins / monthly reviews / quarterly strategy adjustments.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **the right KPIs align the team on what matters**. When everyone is looking at the same 8-12 metrics, conversations shift from "let me find a number that supports my view" to "the dashboard says X; here's what we should do." Picking wrong KPIs means everyone optimises for the wrong things; picking right KPIs makes the team self-correcting.

Second, **operating cadence turns metrics into decisions**. A dashboard reviewed never is shelfware. A dashboard reviewed weekly catches problems early; a monthly review steers operations; a quarterly review steers strategy. The cadence is what produces decisions; the dashboard is just the surface.

Third, **good KPI tracking saves analysis time**. Weekly anomaly review surfaces 80% of the issues that would otherwise require deep traffic analysis. The deep analysis becomes a quarterly investment, not a monthly fire drill.

For Field & Sun: 4 hours of upfront KPI selection + dashboard build + cadence-establishment is plausibly worth more than 40 hours of ad-hoc reporting over the year.

## Core concepts

- **The KPI hierarchy.** Three levels: *executive KPIs* (revenue, conversion rate, organic share — what the board sees), *operating KPIs* (cluster traffic, refresh velocity, decay rate — what the marketing team manages), *diagnostic KPIs* (per-page CTR, schema validation rate, indexing coverage — what specialists track). Each level needs different cadence and audience.
- **Vanity vs business KPIs.** Sessions, pageviews, impressions are vanity unless they ladder up to revenue / conversion / cluster-strategy. KPIs that don't connect to business outcomes get ignored after the first month.
- **Leading vs lagging indicators.** Position changes lead clicks (lead by 1-3 weeks). Clicks lead revenue (lead by 0-4 weeks). Lagging KPIs (revenue) are what stakeholders care about; leading KPIs (rank, impressions, CWV) are what operators steer by.
- **Cluster-level KPIs > page-level KPIs.** Page-level metrics are too granular for executive reporting. Cluster-level rollup is where strategic insight lives.
- **Cadence.** Weekly (15-30 min, alert + top-mover review), monthly (1-2 hr, KPI review + decay flag), quarterly (3-4 hr, strategic review + KPI recalibration). Annual (half-day, KPI-set review).
- **Targets vs trackers.** A KPI without a target is a tracker. A KPI with a target is a commitment. Most brands under-set targets — "we'll aim higher" is not a target.
- **Single source of truth.** The dashboard is the source of truth; ad-hoc reports referencing the same metrics with slightly different numbers create chaos. Pick one platform (Looker Studio is most common), reconcile against source-of-truth, and stick with it.
- **Stakeholder relevance.** The board KPI dashboard is different from the SEO-team operating dashboard. Audience-tailored views of the same data, not separate datasets.
- **KPI drift.** KPIs decay in usefulness. Quarterly review removes / adds / recalibrates. KPIs that haven't been touched in 12 months are usually wrong.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun's marketing team has GA4, GSC, rank tracking, and Shopify all flowing into Looker Studio, but the dashboards are sprawling — 40+ charts across 8 tabs, used by maybe 3 people. The team wants to consolidate to a focused KPI set with clear targets.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `define-seo-kpis`. Skill produces the KPI selection, target setting, dashboard design, and cadence proposal.
>
> **Step 2.** Output:
>
> - **Executive KPIs (5):** organic-search revenue (target $X/quarter), organic % of total revenue (target 35%), organic conversion rate (target 1.8%), branded vs non-branded revenue split (track non-branded growth), share of voice on priority cluster head terms (target +5pp QoQ).
> - **Operating KPIs (8):** cluster sessions (top 6 priority clusters), pillar-page positions (top 3), CTR on top-15-position queries (CTR-opportunity tracker), decayed-page count (>30% YoY decline), refresh velocity (refreshes shipped / quarter), schema validation pass rate, CWV mobile good %, GSC clicks YoY.
> - **Diagnostic KPIs (12):** per-page conversion rates, broken-link count, indexing coverage rate, average page load time, etc. (specialist-level only).
> - **Dashboard structure:** 1-page Executive view, 1-page Operating view, drill-down tabs for diagnostics. Looker Studio.
> - **Cadence:** Weekly (Monday, 30 min, marketing team) + Monthly (first Tuesday, 90 min, marketing + leadership) + Quarterly (3-hr deep review).
> - **Targets:** documented per KPI; refreshed at the quarterly review.
>
> **Step 3.** Implementation: 6 hours to build the new dashboard; 1 hour to brief the team on the new cadence; 30 minutes per week ongoing.
>
> **Step 4.** Result: 90 days later, weekly review attendance is 100% (vs ~50% before); monthly meetings produce documented decisions (vs free-form discussion); quarterly board reports are crisp 5-KPI updates instead of 40-chart slogs. The team catches an indexing issue in week 2 of a refresh sweep that previously would have been visible only at month-end.

## How to do it

1. **Inventory the current state.** What dashboards exist? What metrics are in use? Who reviews them at what cadence? Most brands over-track and under-review.
2. **Pull SEO goals.** From `seo_foundation.md`, with revenue model context from `business_context.md`. KPIs ladder up to goals; goals not in the foundation means KPIs aren't anchored.
3. **Run `define-seo-kpis`.** Skill produces: KPI hierarchy (executive / operating / diagnostic), targets per KPI, dashboard structure, cadence proposal, ownership.
4. **Build the dashboard.** Looker Studio is the standard for most brands; Tableau / PowerBI for enterprise. The dashboard reads from GA4 / GSC / rank-tracker / Shopify directly via connectors or BigQuery.
5. **Set explicit targets.** Per KPI, per quarter. Without targets, KPIs are trackers, not commitments.
6. **Document ownership.** Who owns each KPI? Who reviews it at what cadence? Without ownership, KPIs drift.
7. **Establish cadence.** Calendar the weekly / monthly / quarterly reviews with attendees and agenda templates.
8. **Run the first month.** Most teams over-set initially and then trim back at the quarterly review. That's normal.
9. **Quarterly recalibration.** Review which KPIs are useful, which got ignored, which moved targets. Add / remove / adjust.
10. **Annual KPI-set review.** Bigger overhaul. Strategic shifts (new product line, new market, new business model) often warrant new KPI sets.

## Common pitfalls

- **Too many KPIs.** Dashboards with 30+ KPIs get ignored. 8-12 at the operating level; 5 at the executive level is the right ceiling.
- **No targets.** KPIs without targets aren't KPIs; they're trackers. Add target columns.
- **Vanity metrics.** Sessions / pageviews / impressions in isolation. Connect to revenue / conversion / cluster-strategy or remove.
- **Wrong audience.** Showing the board operating-level diagnostic KPIs (schema validation rate) is theatre. Show executive KPIs.
- **No cadence.** Dashboards reviewed only when stakeholders ask. Set the rhythm.
- **Dashboard drift.** Six months later, the dashboard has 47 charts instead of 12. Tooling-debt review.
- **Disagreeing numbers across reports.** Different reports referencing the same KPI with different numbers = chaos. Single source of truth + reconciled.
- **Ignoring leading indicators.** Tracking only lagging revenue means you find out about decay 3 months late. Position / impressions / CWV are leading.
- **Annual targets without quarterly milestones.** "Annual organic revenue target $X" without quarterly checkpoints is a target that gets evaluated once, post-mortem.
- **No quarterly recalibration.** KPIs decay; the set must evolve.
