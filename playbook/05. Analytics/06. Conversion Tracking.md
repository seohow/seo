# Conversion Tracking

> Connect SEO to revenue. The discipline of measuring what visits actually do — purchase, sign up, lead, contact — and crediting that value to the channels and pages that produced it. Without conversion tracking, SEO is "we drove traffic"; with it, SEO is "we drove $X."

## What it is

Conversion tracking is the practice of capturing meaningful user actions (purchases, sign-ups, lead form submits, account creations) and attributing them to the marketing touchpoints that contributed. In SEO context, it's how organic search work translates into revenue numbers that survive executive scrutiny — "the sun-care cluster contributed $94k in attributed revenue last quarter" rather than "organic traffic is up." Conversion tracking spans event implementation (the data layer / GTM / direct GA4 firing), conversion definition (which events count as goals), attribution modelling (how credit is split across touchpoints), and revenue measurement (per channel, per page, per cluster).

For Field & Sun, conversion tracking is the layer that connects every other SEO category to the business P&L. Without it, every cluster, pillar, refresh, and schema deployment is an aspirational investment. With it, the team can credibly say "this cluster shipped X content; here's the revenue it produced; here's the ROI on the next investment."

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **conversion tracking is what makes SEO accountable to the business**. "Organic traffic is up" without conversion data is unaccountable; conversion data with channel attribution is. Brands that invest in clean conversion tracking can defend SEO budgets; brands that don't get cut.

Second, **broken conversion tracking is the single most common analytics issue**. Most brands have at least one material issue: events double-firing, ecommerce parameters missing, attribution-model mismatches, consent-mode under-counting. Audit before trusting.

Third, **attribution choice materially changes the SEO revenue story**. Last-click usually under-credits SEO (which often plays an upstream role); DDA usually credits it more accurately. Choosing the right model — and being explicit about it — is part of the measurement discipline.

For Field & Sun: a 1-day conversion-tracking audit before any major SEO investment review is plausibly worth more than the next month of dashboard work.

## Core concepts

- **Events vs conversions.** Events are user interactions (page views, scrolls, clicks, form submits); conversions are the events the business cares about (purchase, lead, sign-up). In GA4, conversions are events marked as "key events."
- **Goal events.** Different businesses have different goal events. Ecommerce: `purchase`. SaaS: `sign_up`, `start_trial`, `subscribe`. B2B services: `generate_lead`, `book_meeting`. Marketplaces: `list_item`, `transaction`. Match conversions to business model.
- **Funnel events.** Events that lead to conversions but aren't conversions themselves: `view_item`, `add_to_cart`, `begin_checkout`, `view_cart`, `add_to_wishlist`. Tracking the funnel lets you find drop-off points.
- **Attribution models.** Last-click (100% credit to last paid/organic touch), first-click (100% to first), linear (equal across touches), position-based / U-shaped, time-decay, data-driven (DDA — Google's algorithm splits credit based on observed contribution patterns). DDA is GA4's default.
- **Cross-device and cross-session.** Most conversions involve multiple sessions / devices. Attribution depends on the platform's ability to stitch users across sessions; cookie-based (degrading), Google-account-based (best in Google ecosystem), customer-ID-based (best in CRM context).
- **Consent mode v2 (EU).** Without it, EU users who reject tracking don't appear in conversion data; under-attribution to all channels including SEO.
- **Server-side tracking.** Browser-side (GTM Web) is increasingly limited by browser privacy / ad blockers; server-side (GTM Server) recovers some of that signal. Recommended for ecom brands at scale.
- **Triangulation against source-of-truth.** GA4 conversion count vs Shopify / payment processor count: should match within 5%. Larger gaps require investigation.
- **Multi-touch attribution dashboards.** Show the *path* of touchpoints (first click → ... → purchase). Useful for understanding SEO's role even when last-click doesn't credit it.
- **Custom dimensions for cluster attribution.** Tagging conversions with the cluster the visitor came from (via landing page → cluster mapping) enables cluster-level revenue reporting.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun reports organic-search revenue monthly using GA4's last-click attribution. The number is ~$124k for the recent month. Stakeholders accept the number, but it's not connected to specific clusters / pages, and the team suspects under-counting from broken events + EU consent issues.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `audit-conversion-tracking`. Skill audits event implementation, conversion definitions, ecommerce parameters, attribution model, EU consent, source-of-truth triangulation, and cluster-attribution capability.
>
> **Step 2.** Findings:
>
> - **Event implementation:** `purchase` fires correctly but missing the `items` parameter on ~15% of orders (custom checkout flow doesn't populate items array). Product-level revenue understated by 10-15%.
> - **Conversion definition:** `add_to_cart` is incorrectly marked as a conversion alongside `purchase`. Inflates conversion count.
> - **Attribution:** GA4 default is DDA but Looker Studio reports use last-click. The $124k organic figure is last-click; DDA-credited number is $147k (+19%).
> - **Consent mode:** v2 partially implemented; ~12% of EU users not tracked properly. Under-counts EU conversions.
> - **Source-of-truth triangulation:** GA4 reports $124k vs Shopify $146k → 15% discrepancy (mostly explained by consent + missing `items`).
> - **Cluster attribution:** no cluster custom dimension; cluster-level revenue currently impossible.
>
> **Step 3.** Fix list:
>
> - Fix `items` parameter on custom checkout flow (3-5 days dev).
> - Un-mark `add_to_cart` as conversion (30 min).
> - Standardise on DDA attribution; document in reports (4 hrs).
> - Complete Consent Mode v2 (1-2 days dev).
> - Add cluster custom dimension via landing-page → cluster mapping in BigQuery (2-3 days analytics).
>
> **Step 4.** Result: 30 days post-fix, GA4 organic-search revenue matches Shopify within 4%. DDA reports show $158k organic revenue (+27% vs the broken last-click report). Cluster-level attribution enables reporting per cluster — sun-care cluster contributes $94k, vs the previous "we don't know."

## How to do it

1. **Audit current state.** Run `audit-conversion-tracking`.
2. **Fix P0 issues first.** Broken events, missing parameters, wrong conversion definitions.
3. **Standardise attribution.** Pick a model (DDA recommended for most brands); document; align downstream reports.
4. **Implement Consent Mode v2 (EU/UK).** Required.
5. **Triangulate quarterly.** GA4 vs Shopify / source-of-truth; flag any discrepancy >5%.
6. **Add cluster attribution.** Landing-page → cluster mapping (via BigQuery or custom dimension). Enables cluster-level revenue reporting.
7. **Build server-side tracking** (large brands). Recovers signal lost to browser privacy.
8. **Cross-reference with rank tracking + GSC.** Conversion data alone misses upstream SEO contribution; multi-touch dashboards close the loop.
9. **Re-audit quarterly.** Conversion tracking drifts; new site features, new events, new platforms all change the picture.
10. **Connect to KPI dashboard.** Conversion data feeds executive + operating KPIs.

## Common pitfalls

- **Trusting conversion data without auditing.** Most brands have at least one material issue; audit first.
- **Last-click reporting only.** Under-credits SEO contribution. Run DDA reports in parallel.
- **No source-of-truth triangulation.** GA4-only conversion numbers can drift silently.
- **Missing `items` parameter.** Most common ecommerce issue; breaks product-level revenue analysis.
- **`add_to_cart` marked as conversion.** Inflates count.
- **Consent Mode v2 not implemented (EU).** 10-30% under-counting; legally required.
- **No cluster attribution.** Can't report cluster-level revenue without it.
- **Server-side tracking absent (large ecom).** Browser-privacy + ad-blockers cost ~10-20% of conversions in browser-only setups.
- **Conversion-tracking drift.** Fixed once and ignored. Quarterly re-audit catches drift.
- **Multiple attribution models in different reports.** Reports disagreeing on the same metric = chaos. Single model + documented.
