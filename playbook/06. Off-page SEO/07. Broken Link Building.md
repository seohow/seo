# Broken Link Building

> Finding broken outbound links on other sites and pitching the brand's content as the replacement. A specific tactical pattern with predictable yield — slower than PR, more reliable than guest posting.

## What it is

Broken link building is a tactical channel within link building: the brand identifies pages on other sites that have broken outbound links (404s, dead domains), checks whether the brand has a topically relevant page that could replace the broken target, and pitches the publisher to swap the broken link for the working one. Done at scale, it produces a steady flow of backlinks at moderate effort. Typical conversion: 5-12% (which is meaningfully higher than cold outreach because the publisher *already needs* a fix).

For Field & Sun, broken-link building is a useful supplement to higher-yield channels. A disciplined programme produces 10-15 placements per quarter at moderate effort, mostly Tier 2-3. The placements aren't usually Tier 1 (Tier 1 publishers fix their own broken links faster than a brand can find them), but the volume + steady cadence makes it a reliable contributor to the off-page mix.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **broken-link building solves a real problem for the publisher**. Unlike cold outreach where the publisher has to be convinced of a benefit, broken-link pitches highlight an existing problem (the broken link makes the article worse) and offer the fix. Conversion rates reflect this: 5-12% vs. 1-3% on cold pitches.

Second, **it's predictable**. Once the prospecting pattern is set up (find broken-link-rich pages → identify replacement assets → pitch), the channel produces a steady cadence. Predictable cadence is rare in off-page work; it makes broken-link building useful for filling gaps between high-variance PR campaigns.

Third, **it earns topically relevant links**. The replacement assets are aligned to the original target's topic — the resulting links are contextual and editorially placed, which counts more than random link drops.

The trade-offs: broken-link building is slow per-pitch (10-30 minutes per prospect once the pattern is established) and ceiling-bound. Brands that attempt to scale it past 30-50 placements per quarter usually find diminishing returns. It's a 15-25% slot in the off-page mix, not a primary channel.

## Core concepts

- **Prospecting pattern.** Find resource pages, listicles, or roundups in your category. Run a broken-link checker on them. Identify candidates where the broken target was on a topic your brand covers.
- **Replacement asset match.** The brand's replacement page must be topically equivalent to the broken target. Not "we have a page in this category" — actually-equivalent ("the broken link was to a guide on mineral sunscreen application; our replacement is our pillar on the same topic").
- **Wayback Machine.** Used to verify what the broken target *used to be*. Confirms topical match.
- **Link-checker tools.** Ahrefs Site Explorer (broken-outbound-links report), Semrush, Check My Links (Chrome extension), Dr. Link Check, Sitechecker.
- **Resource pages.** "Best [topic] resources" / "Useful links for [topic]" — these are broken-link gold mines because they aggregate dozens of outbound links.
- **Pitch craft.** Polite + specific + helpful. Lead with the broken link (the publisher's problem); offer the replacement (your page); short.
- **Yield ceiling.** 10-30 placements per month is realistic for a small team; beyond that, prospecting becomes inefficient.
- **Anchor text.** Mostly under brand control if pitch is accepted. Use branded / partial-match; avoid pure exact-match.
- **Avoid spam.** Some broken-link "tools" send templated mass emails; this damages sender reputation. Personalise + verify topical match.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun has a working outreach process and is filling channels. Broken-link building is part of the link-building plan but hasn't been run systematically. The team wants to set up a quarter's worth of campaigns.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `plan-broken-link-campaign`. Skill produces the prospecting + outreach plan.
>
> **Step 2.** Output:
>
> - **Prospect categories:** "skincare resources," "sun protection guide," "dermatologist resources," "mineral sunscreen list," "sun-care ingredient resources" (etc.).
> - **Tooling:** Ahrefs Site Explorer for broken-outbound-link discovery + Wayback Machine for content verification + Lemlist for sending.
> - **Asset inventory:** mineral-sunscreen pillar (high replacement value), evergreen "what is mineral sunscreen" anchor, three blog spokes.
> - **Targets:** ~50 prospect pages identified per week of prospecting; ~8-15 broken-link matches; 5-12 conversions/quarter.
> - **Pitch template:** specific, polite, 3-paragraph max.
> - **Cadence:** 3-4 hrs prospecting per week + 2 hrs outreach.
> - **Tracking:** placements logged in CRM; conversion-rate review monthly.
>
> **Step 3.** Result: Q2 ships 14 broken-link conversions (target 10-15); 11 are Tier 2-3 placements; 3 are Tier 1-adjacent (DR 60s). Steady-state for the channel.

## How to do it

1. **Identify replacement assets.** Pillar pages, evergreen anchors, comparison pages, tools — anything that could substitute for a similar page on another site.
2. **Build prospect categories.** Search queries that surface "[category] resources," "[category] roundups," "best [category] guides" — pages likely to have broken outbound links.
3. **Run broken-link discovery.** Per prospect URL: Ahrefs Site Explorer (broken-outbound-links report) or Check My Links (Chrome). Filter the broken targets to ones that were topically aligned with the brand.
4. **Verify topical match (Wayback Machine).** Was the broken target genuinely about [topic]? Don't pitch unrelated replacements.
5. **Build outreach list.** Per qualifying prospect: page URL, broken link URL, original (Wayback) target, brand's replacement, target editor / contact.
6. **Send personalised pitches.** Lead with the broken link, offer the replacement, keep short.
7. **Follow up.** 1-2 follow-ups across 7-10 days.
8. **Track placements.** Per CRM; monthly conversion-rate review.
9. **Iterate.** Some prospect categories produce yield; others don't. Rebalance.
10. **Run quarterly review.** Channel performance vs other channels.

## Common pitfalls

- **No topical-match check.** Pitching a Field & Sun mineral-sunscreen page as a replacement for a broken vegan-baking-recipe link is spam. Verify match.
- **Mass-templated outreach.** Tools that send templated broken-link pitches at volume earn 0.5% conversion + sender-reputation damage.
- **Skipping Wayback verification.** The broken link's *current* state isn't the topic — it's whatever moved / 404'd. Wayback shows what was there.
- **Pitching homepage replacements.** The broken target was a specific resource; replacement should be a specific resource page (pillar / evergreen / tool), not the brand's homepage.
- **Over-investing.** Broken-link building scales poorly past 30-50 placements/month. Cap effort.
- **Forgetting follow-ups.** Most placements come from follow-up 1 (Day 4-5). Skipping = half yield.
- **Wrong contact.** Editor / webmaster / contact-page form > generic "info@."
- **Anchor-text exact-match.** Even with brand control, mix anchors; pure exact-match is a manipulation signal.
- **No placement tracking.** Without data, channel can't be optimised.
- **Pitching low-quality / suspect target sites.** Some broken-link-rich pages are low-quality / spam-network. Skip.
