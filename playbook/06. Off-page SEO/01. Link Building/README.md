# Link Building

> The strategic layer of off-page work — which links matter, which assets earn them, how to plan a campaign tied to clusters and business goals. Not a tactic, a programme.

## What it is

Link building is the practice of earning backlinks from other websites to your own. Each backlink is a *vote* of authority: another site is saying "this page is worth linking to." Google (and increasingly LLM-driven search) interprets the aggregate link graph as a measure of which pages — and which sites — are authoritative on a topic. Link building is the set of strategies, assets, and operational disciplines that earn those votes systematically.

For Field & Sun, link building is the lift that turns "cluster-mapped, well-structured content" into "cluster-mapped, well-structured content that *ranks*." Without a link-building programme, the brand will plateau at positions 8-15 on most competitive sun-care queries — beaten by Wirecutter, NYT, EWG, and competitors with more authority. With a programme — pillar-page links, original-data PR, dermatologist citations — the brand can break into top-5 on its priority terms.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **backlinks are still one of Google's strongest ranking signals**. Google has rebalanced over years (E-E-A-T, content quality, helpful content, AI signals all matter more now), but links remain a top-3 factor for competitive queries. Off-page is not optional for brands competing in saturated verticals.

Second, **links compound**. A single high-quality link from a publisher (NYT, Wirecutter, BBC) lifts the ranking of the page *and* the site authority *and* makes the next link easier to earn (link-attracting effect). The compounding works in your favour if you build durable links to durable assets; it works against you if you build cheap links to thin pages.

Third, **link building is the most-faked SEO category**. Vendors selling "200 backlinks for $99," PBN networks, link farms, and exchange schemes all promise quick wins. Each of these has Google detection signals; each has destroyed brands when the penalty hits. Doing link building well — slowly, on real-asset foundations, with quality outreach — is harder and slower than doing it badly. The brands that do it well compound; the ones that shortcut get penalised.

For Field & Sun: a quarterly link-building plan focused on pillar pages, original data, and dermatologist citations is plausibly worth more in 18-month organic-revenue lift than any single content investment of equivalent effort.

## Core concepts

- **Link-worthy assets.** Pages other people will naturally link to. Pillar pages (definitive guides), original research (industry surveys, brand-published data), tools (calculators, quizzes, finders), evergreen anchor pieces, comparison pages, and newsworthy stories. Without link-worthy assets, no amount of outreach earns links.
- **Domain rating / domain authority (DR / DA).** Approximate measures of a site's link profile (Ahrefs uses DR; Moz uses DA; Semrush uses Authority Score). A link from a DR-70+ site is worth substantially more than one from a DR-20 site. Quality > quantity.
- **Anchor text.** The clickable text of the link. Mix of branded ("Field & Sun"), naked URL ("fieldandsun.com"), partial-match ("guide to mineral sunscreen"), exact-match ("mineral sunscreen guide"). Over-optimisation (high % of exact-match anchors) is a manipulation signal.
- **Link relevance.** Topically relevant links (skincare publication → skincare brand) are worth more than irrelevant links (tech blog → skincare brand). Relevance increasingly matters as Google's models get better at understanding context.
- **Editorial vs. paid links.** Editorial links (a journalist chose to link) are gold. Paid links (sponsored content, link-insertion services) are at minimum risky and at worst penalty-triggering. Editorial-only is the right policy for serious brands.
- **Link velocity.** The rate at which new links are acquired. Sudden spikes (50 links in a week to a fresh domain) look manipulated; sustained growth (5-15 links/month) looks natural.
- **`nofollow` / `sponsored` / `ugc` attributes.** Links can be marked with these to signal Google not to count them as endorsements. Most journalist links are followed (no attribute); paid / sponsored / user-generated links are usually not. Mix is normal.
- **Link diversity.** A natural link profile has varied sources — publishers, blogs, university pages, government pages, niche forums, comparison sites, newsletters. A profile with 90% of links from blog comments + forums is suspicious.
- **Cluster-level link strategy.** Don't think "build links to the site"; think "build links to cluster X's pillar to lift cluster X's rankings." Cluster-level focus produces measurable outcomes; site-level produces vanity metrics.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun has shipped the sun-care cluster (mineral sunscreen pillar + 8 spokes), the skin-type-finder quiz, and 3 evergreen anchor pieces. The brand is well-positioned for content but has only ~145 referring domains site-wide; competitors have 800-2,500. The team wants a 12-month link-building plan.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `plan-link-building-campaign`. Skill produces the strategic plan covering target assets, target sites, tactical channels, cadence, and success metrics.
>
> **Step 2.** Output:
>
> - **Target assets (the link destinations):** mineral-sunscreen pillar (priority 1), evergreen "what is mineral sunscreen" anchor (priority 1), skin-type quiz (priority 1), 3 high-impressions blog spokes (priority 2).
> - **Target sites (the link sources):**
>   - **Tier 1 (DR 70+, ~30 sites):** Wirecutter, NYT Wirecutter, Cosmopolitan, Allure, Byrdie, Real Simple. Earned via digital PR + dermatologist commentary.
>   - **Tier 2 (DR 50-70, ~80 sites):** specialised skincare publications, dermatologist blogs, sustainability/wellness publications.
>   - **Tier 3 (DR 30-50, ~150 sites):** niche skincare blogs, parenting-and-skin sites, regional health publications.
> - **Tactical channels:** digital PR (3 campaigns), HARO (ongoing), guest posting (4 placements/quarter), broken-link building (10-15 links/quarter), brand-mention recovery (5-10 links/quarter).
> - **Cadence:** target 8-15 new referring domains/month for 12 months.
> - **Success metrics:** referring domains 145 → 280; pillar position +3; sun-care cluster non-branded organic traffic +60%.
>
> **Step 3.** The team executes against the plan. Not all tactics earn equal yield: digital PR produces 2 Tier-1 placements + 6 Tier-2 placements in Q1 (massive impact); HARO produces 1 Tier-2 + 3 Tier-3 placements; broken-link building produces ~15 Tier-3 placements/quarter; guest posting under-performs (slow, hard to find quality publications accepting contributors).
>
> **Step 4.** Result: 12 months later, referring domains have grown to 305 (vs target 280); pillar position is 6 (vs 11 baseline); sun-care cluster non-branded organic +72%. The PR-led campaigns drove most of the lift; broken-link building was steady; guest posting was deprioritised in Q3-Q4.

## How to do it

1. **Audit current state.** Pull current referring domains (Ahrefs / Semrush / GSC Links report). Note count, DR distribution, top-linking sites, anchor-text mix.
2. **Compare to competitor link profiles.** Top 3-5 competitors. Identify the gap (typically 5-20x referring domains for emerging brands). The gap is the campaign target.
3. **Identify link-worthy assets.** Pillar pages, evergreen pieces, tools, original data, comparison content. If the brand doesn't have these, link-building outreach won't earn much; build the assets first.
4. **Plan the campaign with `plan-link-building-campaign`.** Strategic plan: target assets, target sites (3-tier), tactical channels (PR / guest / HARO / broken / mention recovery), cadence, metrics.
5. **Build the prospect list.** Per tactical channel. Specific sites + target contact + reason-to-link.
6. **Set up outreach operations.** Email templates, CRM, follow-up cadence (covered in `05. Outreach`).
7. **Execute the tactical channels.**
   - Digital PR (`plan-digital-pr-campaign`): newsworthy angles, original data, expert commentary.
   - HARO / journalist outreach (`set-up-haro-response-system`): reactive opportunities.
   - Guest posting (`plan-guest-posting-campaign`): contributing to publications.
   - Broken-link building (`plan-broken-link-campaign`): replacement pitches.
   - Brand-mention recovery (`monitor-brand-mentions`): unlinked → linked conversion.
8. **Monitor + measure.** Referring-domain growth, anchor-text distribution, link-velocity (sustained, not spiked), cluster-level ranking lift.
9. **Iterate quarterly.** Some tactics work for some brands; others don't. Learn from the data; rebalance the mix.
10. **Watch for toxic links.** Spam links, link-farm patterns, irrelevant Tier-3 sites at high volume — disavow if needed (rare; mostly Google handles automatically now).

## Common pitfalls

- **Buying links.** Don't. Google detects; penalties are severe; the downside outweighs the lift.
- **PBN (private blog network) usage.** Same risk profile as buying. Avoid.
- **Spam-volume outreach.** Bulk-templated emails to thousands of sites: 0.5-1% conversion, damaged sender reputation, reputational risk. Quality > quantity.
- **No link-worthy assets.** Outreach without assets is begging. Build pillars / tools / evergreen / original data first.
- **Anchor-text over-optimisation.** High % of exact-match anchors. Mix branded, naked URL, partial-match, exact-match.
- **Sudden link velocity spikes.** 50 links in 2 weeks looks manipulated. Sustained, organic-looking growth is the goal.
- **Linking only to homepage.** Most brands' link profiles over-concentrate on homepage. Cluster-page targeting (pillars + tools + evergreen) lifts more rankings.
- **Ignoring `nofollow` quality.** A `nofollow` link from a Tier-1 publisher (NYT, Wirecutter) still drives referral traffic, brand visibility, and indirect ranking signal. Don't dismiss because of the attribute.
- **No cluster-level focus.** Site-level link counts are vanity; cluster-level ranking lift is operational.
- **No measurement of off-page → ranking lift.** Without measurement, the budget gets cut. Tie link-building output to ranking + traffic outcomes per cluster.
