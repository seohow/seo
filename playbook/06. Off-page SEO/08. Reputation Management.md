# Reputation Management

> Managing the branded SERP and broader online reputation. Defensive but increasingly strategic — branded reputation feeds AI Overviews, LLM citations, and conversion-rate. The work that protects what every other SEO category has built.

## What it is

Reputation management is the practice of monitoring and shaping how the brand appears in search results when people search for the brand name (or its variants), and across the broader web of reviews, social mentions, and AI-search surfaces. Page 1 of "Field & Sun" search results, the AI Overview that surfaces when someone asks an LLM about Field & Sun, third-party review aggregator content, news mentions, and competitor-attack content (when they exist) — all are reputation surfaces. Reputation management is the operational work of monitoring, intervening, and shaping these surfaces.

For Field & Sun, reputation management is largely defensive at the brand's current scale (no major incidents). But the surfaces that define brand reputation in 2026 — AI Overviews, LLM citations, third-party review aggregators, social-mention-trends — are increasingly determinative for conversion rate, partner relationships, and customer trust. A clean branded SERP + favourable AI Overview produces higher conversion than the same site with a chaotic branded SERP. The work is preventative more than reactive — done well, it ensures crises don't materialise; done badly, it amplifies issues that should have been caught early.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **branded SERP is the highest-CTR search result the brand will ever earn**. Customers searching "Field & Sun" or "Field and Sun reviews" are high-intent — they're researching whether to buy. Page 1 of those queries determines conversion rate as much as any landing-page optimisation. A clean branded SERP with the brand's pages dominating positions 1-5 + favourable third-party content is conversion-positive; a messy SERP with negative review aggregators / AI Overview repeating mis-information is conversion-negative.

Second, **AI Overviews + LLM citation surfaces are reputation surfaces now**. When customers ask ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini "Is Field & Sun trustworthy?" or "Does Field & Sun work for sensitive skin?", the LLM's answer pulls from web content the brand may not control. Branded reputation work increasingly includes shaping the AI-response layer, not just the SERP layer.

Third, **defensive work is cheap if proactive, expensive if reactive**. Catching a reputation issue at the "first negative review on a Tier 2 publication" stage is fixable with a polite editor outreach. Catching it at the "page 1 of branded SERP is dominated by negative content + AI Overviews repeat the criticism" stage requires months of damage-control work. Quarterly monitoring + early-intervention discipline is dramatically cheaper.

## Core concepts

- **Branded SERP.** Page 1 of "[brand name]" search results. Should be dominated by brand-owned properties (homepage, key landing pages, product pages, blog) + favourable third-party content (positive reviews, press placements). Negative content on page 1 is a problem.
- **Branded SERP variants.** "[brand] reviews," "[brand] vs [competitor]," "is [brand] trustworthy," "[brand] complaints" — each variant is a different SERP. Monitor all priority variants.
- **AI Overview / LLM response surface.** What the major LLMs (ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini / Perplexity) say about the brand when asked. Pulls from public web content + training data. Distinct from search SERP.
- **Reputation tiers.**
  - Tier 1: brand-owned (high-control).
  - Tier 2: positive third-party (medium-control via outreach + relationships).
  - Tier 3: neutral third-party (low-control).
  - Tier 4: negative third-party (intervention case-by-case).
- **Defensive content strategy.** Building owned content that ranks for branded SERP variants — particularly "[brand] vs," "is [brand]," "[brand] reviews." Limits competitor / negative content visibility.
- **Review platforms.** Trustpilot, Reviews.io, Google Reviews, Yelp, BBB, etc. Some matter more for branded SERP visibility; managing the highest-visibility platforms is part of reputation work.
- **Negative content response.** Range of responses — ignore, engage politely, request correction, legal escalation. Most negative content should be ignored or politely engaged; escalation is rare and risky.
- **AI Overview shaping.** Shaping what LLMs say about the brand requires producing authoritative content on relevant topics + ensuring the content is cited / linked in surfaces LLMs train on.
- **Crisis escalation paths.** Branded-SERP audit → reputation issue identified → response decision → execution → monitoring. Document the path; don't improvise during a crisis.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun has not done formal reputation work. The branded SERP looks fine intuitively but no one has audited systematically. The team wants a quarterly cadence.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `audit-branded-serp`. Skill produces the branded-SERP audit + reputation plan.
>
> **Step 2.** Output:
>
> - **Branded SERP audit:**
>   - Position 1-5: brand homepage, About page, Mineral Sun Drops PDP, blog post, Instagram. Healthy.
>   - Position 6-10: Trustpilot review (mostly positive but one prominent negative review at top), Cosmopolitan article (positive), Reddit thread (mixed sentiment), Wikipedia (none — flag), competitor's "vs" page (neutral; could be mitigated).
> - **AI Overview probe:** asked ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity 8 questions about Field & Sun. Responses generally accurate; one Gemini response mistakenly attributed a competitor's product line to Field & Sun (correctable via authoritative content publishing).
> - **Branded variants audit:** "Field & Sun reviews" (decent), "Field & Sun vs Supergoop" (competitor's page wins; brand should publish own version), "is Field & Sun trustworthy" (only Trustpilot ranks; brand should consider publishing a transparency / methodology page).
> - **Defensive content gaps:** no brand-owned "vs Supergoop" comparison; no "About Field & Sun's Methodology" page; no Wikipedia presence.
> - **Recommended actions:** ship 2 defensive content pages (vs Supergoop comparison; transparency / methodology overview); engage with one negative Trustpilot review; consider Wikipedia draft (long lead time).
> - **Quarterly cadence:** audit every 90 days; flag changes; act.
>
> **Step 3.** Result: 90 days post-action — branded SERP cleaner (defensive pages rank for variants previously dominated by competitors); AI Overview accuracy improved (shaping content published); Trustpilot interaction improved overall sentiment.

## How to do it

1. **Audit the branded SERP.** Page 1 of "[brand]" + variants ("reviews," "vs [competitor]," "is [brand] trustworthy," "[brand] complaints").
2. **Audit AI surfaces.** Ask the major LLMs branded questions; capture responses; check for inaccuracies / outdated info.
3. **Audit review platforms.** Highest-visibility (typically Trustpilot, Google, Yelp) plus category-specific.
4. **Categorise the surfaces.** Tier 1-4. Identify gaps + risks.
5. **Plan defensive content.** Brand-owned pages that should rank for branded variants — "vs Competitor" comparison, "About / Methodology" overview, "Reviews" (if owned), "FAQ."
6. **Plan AI-Overview shaping content.** Authoritative pages that LLMs can cite (founder bio, methodology, dermatologist advisor profile, brand fact sheet).
7. **Plan response handling.** Triage rules for negative content (ignore / engage / escalate).
8. **Establish quarterly cadence.** Audit every 90 days; flag changes.
9. **Document escalation paths.** Crisis-response procedure; who decides what; legal-counsel triggers.
10. **Connect to other categories.** Brand-mention monitoring (linked / unlinked), digital PR (positive coverage), Content SEO (defensive content publishing).

## Common pitfalls

- **No audit ever.** Branded SERP shifts; without monitoring, problems aren't caught until they're customer-visible.
- **Reactive only.** Waiting for a crisis to hire reputation services; the cost is 10-50x proactive monitoring.
- **Engaging every negative.** Some negative reviews / mentions deserve engagement; many don't. Engaging at scale amplifies + signals defensiveness.
- **Skipping AI surfaces.** AI Overviews / LLM responses are increasingly determinative. Probing them quarterly is now part of reputation work.
- **No defensive content plan.** Branded variants ("vs," "is X trustworthy") should have brand-owned pages ranking. Without them, third parties dominate.
- **Wikipedia obsession.** Wikipedia is hard to enter (notability requirements); don't over-invest unless brand has genuine notability + external press.
- **Legal escalation as default.** Legal threats over negative reviews almost always backfire (Streisand effect). Reserve for genuine defamation; engage politely otherwise.
- **No documented escalation paths.** Crisis improvisation produces mistakes. Document who decides what, with what trigger.
- **Conflating brand-mention monitoring with branded-SERP monitoring.** Different surfaces, different work.
- **Ignoring review platforms.** Trustpilot / Google Reviews appear on branded SERP for many brands; managing them affects SERP visibility.
