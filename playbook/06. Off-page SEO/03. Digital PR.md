# Digital PR

> Earning press coverage, journalist citations, and authority links via newsworthy stories, original data, and expert commentary. The highest-yield off-page channel for most brands — and the most-difficult to do well.

## What it is

Digital PR is the practice of generating editorial coverage in publications via newsworthy story creation, original-data publication, and expert commentary. Unlike guest posting (where the brand writes for the publication) or HARO (where the brand reacts to journalist queries), digital PR is *proactive* — the brand creates an asset (a survey, a report, a study, a commentary angle) and pitches it to journalists. When it lands, it produces premium-publisher links, brand exposure to massive audiences, and a halo effect that lifts branded search and conversion rates.

For Field & Sun, digital PR is plausibly the single highest-leverage off-page channel. A successful campaign — say, a "2026 Sun-Care Survey" with original findings — can earn 8-25 placements at Tier 1-2 publications, each of which counts more than 50 broken-link placements combined. The trade-off: PR campaigns have high variance (some land, some don't), require substantial upfront investment (data + production + outreach + relationship management), and depend on an angle that's genuinely newsworthy. Done well, PR is the off-page channel that defines whether a brand competes; done badly, it's expensive theatre.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **PR-led links are typically the highest-quality links a brand earns**. NYT, Wirecutter, Vogue, BBC, Wall Street Journal — these don't link via outreach to "your business is great" pitches; they link when the brand has something newsworthy to say. PR campaigns are how brands earn into the link tier that matters most.

Second, **PR drives brand awareness alongside SEO value**. A piece in Allure produces SEO benefit (a follow link, contextual relevance) *plus* hundreds of thousands of branded impressions, plus referral traffic, plus a citation reusable in marketing forever. The total value usually exceeds the SEO value alone by 2-5x.

Third, **PR coverage feeds AI Overview and LLM citation signals**. AI search systems disproportionately cite content from authoritative publications — exactly where PR earns coverage. As AI search expands, PR-earned coverage becomes a leading indicator of AI-search visibility.

The trade-off: PR campaigns cost 60-200 hours of effort each (data + production + pitch + relationship management). 1-3 campaigns per year is realistic for most brands. The angle has to be genuinely newsworthy; "we launched a new product" doesn't qualify. Brands that don't invest in PR can compete in some categories; in saturated, premium categories (skincare, finance, parenting), PR is the difference between fighting for top 10 and winning top 5.

## Core concepts

- **Newsworthy angles.** Original data (surveys, internal data, public-source synthesis), expert commentary on news (reactive opportunities), trend analysis, controversy / counter-narrative pieces, "first" claims (first-of-its-kind, first comparable). Not newsworthy: product launches, brand milestones, "we won an award."
- **Original-data PR.** The most-reliable PR pattern: the brand commissions / runs a survey or analyses internal data and publishes findings journalists can cite. Higher upfront cost; higher-yield + reusable for years.
- **Expert commentary PR.** The brand has a credentialed expert (founder + dermatologist + advisor) who comments on news / trends / data publicly. Leverages reactive opportunities; lower upfront cost but requires the expert + the relationship infrastructure.
- **Story creation.** The pitch is the story. Brand-mentioned-in-passing isn't PR. The pitch packages an angle journalists can build a story around, with all the assets (data, quotes, images, stats) ready to use.
- **Embargo + exclusive strategy.** Premium placements often require offering exclusivity — first-look to one Tier 1 publication, then broader release. Exclusivity earns Tier 1 coverage that mass-pitch wouldn't.
- **Relationship infrastructure.** PR works on warm relationships. Cold pitches earn 1-3% conversion; warm relationships earn 30-50%. Building the journalist relationship layer is half the PR programme.
- **Measurement.** PR is hard to measure cleanly. Standard signals: links earned, audience reach, branded-search lift (PR halo), referral traffic, citation reuse in subsequent coverage.
- **Variance.** PR campaigns are high-variance. Some hit Tier 1 in week one; others land 2 Tier 3 placements in 8 weeks. Plan portfolios (3+ campaigns / year), not single bets.
- **Proactive vs. reactive.** Proactive PR = brand creates asset + pitches. Reactive PR = brand responds to journalist queries (handled separately in `06. HARO and Journalist Outreach`). Most strong programmes do both.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun has not done structured digital PR. The team has a dermatologist advisor and ~3,000 customers who could be surveyed. The brand wants to plan one PR campaign for Q2 2026, scoped to test the channel.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `plan-digital-pr-campaign`. Skill produces the campaign plan including angle, data plan, asset list, target publications, pitch strategy, and measurement.
>
> **Step 2.** Output:
>
> - **Angle:** "2026 Sun-Care Habits Report" — survey of 1,000 sunscreen users on application frequency, ingredient awareness, skin-tone concerns; co-authored with dermatologist advisor.
> - **Newsworthy hook:** preliminary findings show 67% of users underestimate the amount of mineral sunscreen needed for facial coverage; 43% have never re-applied during a workday; only 12% know which UV filters their sunscreen contains.
> - **Asset list:** survey methodology doc, raw data spreadsheet (downloadable), 5-7 chartable findings, expert commentary quotes (founder + dermatologist), executive-summary one-pager, hi-res chart images, dermatologist headshot + bio, brand fact sheet.
> - **Target publications (15 Tier 1, 30 Tier 2):** Wirecutter, NYT Wirecutter, Allure, Cosmopolitan, Self, Refinery29, Byrdie, etc. plus dermatologist publications.
> - **Embargo strategy:** offer NYT Wirecutter or Allure exclusive 48-hour first-look; broader release after.
> - **Outreach:** ~50 personalised pitches to journalists who've covered sun-care or skincare-data topics in last 90 days.
> - **Production timeline:** 8 weeks (survey design + run 3 weeks; analysis + report production 3 weeks; pre-launch outreach 2 weeks).
> - **Success criteria:** 1+ Tier 1 placement; 5+ Tier 2 placements; 10+ Tier 3 placements; 10K+ branded impressions in Q2; +5% branded search YoY.
>
> **Step 3.** Result: 12 weeks post-launch — 1 NYT Wirecutter mention (Tier 1, biggest win); 8 Tier 2 placements (Allure, Self, Real Simple, Refinery29, etc.); 14 Tier 3; ~1.2M total readers; +12% branded search lift in the following 90 days. The team plans 2-3 follow-on campaigns over the next 12 months.

## How to do it

1. **Choose the angle archetype.** Original data (highest yield, highest cost); expert commentary (medium); trend / counter-narrative (medium); "first" claim (variable). Match angle to the brand's distinctive capability.
2. **Validate newsworthiness.** Would a journalist write a story about this *if it weren't a brand-funded pitch*? If no, it's not newsworthy.
3. **Run `plan-digital-pr-campaign`.** Produces the campaign plan: angle, data plan, asset list, target publications, pitch strategy, embargo decision, measurement.
4. **Build the asset.** Survey + analysis, internal-data report, expert-commentary script. 4-8 weeks typical.
5. **Build the pitch.** Headline-ready findings, journalist-friendly summary, downloadable data, expert quotes ready, image assets ready.
6. **Decide embargo strategy.** Offer Tier 1 exclusive (48 hours) or broad simultaneous release. Exclusive earns Tier 1; simultaneous earns broader Tier 2-3 reach.
7. **Outreach.** Personalised pitches to journalists who've covered the topic / category. Reference specific recent pieces. Offer the data + expert + interview availability.
8. **Manage the post-launch wave.** Track placements, follow up with non-responders, package follow-on angles for journalists who covered.
9. **Measure.** Placements (count, tier, link quality), audience reach, branded-search lift, referral traffic, follow-on opportunities.
10. **Capture for reuse.** PR data assets get cited for years. Maintain the asset; refresh annually if relevant.

## Common pitfalls

- **Not newsworthy.** "Brand launches new product" / "Brand wins industry award" / "Brand expands to new market" are press releases, not digital PR. Journalists ignore.
- **No data, no story.** Pitches with original data convert at 5-10x the rate of pitches without. Without something journalists can cite, the angle is weak.
- **Wrong target publications.** Pitching tech publications a skincare data report wastes both sides' time. Topical-relevance rules.
- **Spam-volume outreach.** Bulk-templated pitches to 500 journalists earn 0.5% conversion. Personalised pitches to 50 earn 10-20%.
- **No expert / credibility layer.** Brand-only commentary earns weaker placements than founder + dermatologist or founder + researcher commentary.
- **No embargo strategy.** Simultaneous release earns broad Tier 2-3 coverage but rarely Tier 1. Exclusive earns Tier 1 (sometimes).
- **No follow-up plan.** Most placements come from journalists who didn't respond to the first email. 2-3 follow-ups across 2 weeks doubles yield.
- **Over-promising upside.** Some campaigns land Tier 1; some land 0. Plan portfolios (3+ campaigns / year), not single-shot bets.
- **No measurement / attribution.** Without tracking links earned, audience reach, and branded-search lift, the budget gets cut. Document outcomes.
- **Forgetting the asset is reusable.** A 2026 Sun-Care Habits Report is citable in 2027, 2028. Maintain the asset; update annually.
