# Guest Posting

> Writing for other publications. A specific tactical channel within link building, with its own rules — quality of publication, contributor relationships, anchor strategy. Slow but compounding when done at quality publications.

## What it is

Guest posting is the practice of writing original content for publication on someone else's site, typically with a byline and a backlink to your own site. The right guest post earns: (1) a backlink from a topically relevant publication, (2) brand exposure to the publication's audience, (3) a contributor relationship the brand can extend over time, and (4) a writing portfolio that establishes the author / brand as expert. The wrong guest post — published on a low-quality "guest-post farm" or a contributor-network site — earns a flagged link, no audience, and time wasted.

For Field & Sun, guest posting is one of several tactical off-page channels — and not the highest-yield one. The higher-yield channels (digital PR, HARO, broken-link building) tend to dominate; guest posting plays a steady, secondary role. Done at quality publications (Allure contributor, Byrdie expert column, dermatologist-association publications), it produces 1-3 placements per quarter and supports both link building and authority signals.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **a quality guest post earns a contextual link from a topically relevant site**. Topically relevant links are worth meaningfully more than irrelevant ones; a Field & Sun byline in Allure is far more valuable than a generic blog comment from a DR-50 site.

Second, **guest posting builds contributor relationships that compound**. The first placement is the hardest; subsequent placements at the same publication become much easier. Brands that invest in 2-3 contributor relationships at quality publications can earn a steady flow of placements over years.

Third, **guest posting builds author authority signals**. E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) increasingly matters; an author with bylines at credible publications signals expertise to both Google and readers. For YMYL-adjacent categories (skincare, health, finance), author authority is a meaningful ranking factor.

The trade-offs: quality publications are *hard* to get into. Most publications that accept "guest posts" in 2026 are content-mill or low-quality contributor networks; serious publications have editorial-quality bars and most pitches are rejected. Guest posting works as one channel in a portfolio; it's rarely the dominant channel.

## Core concepts

- **Quality publication vs. content-mill.** Quality publications have editorial standards, professional editors, named author bylines, and limited contributor slots. Content mills accept anything, charge "review fees," publish "guest posts" alongside paid sponsored content. Stay in the first category; the second has Google detection signals.
- **Topical relevance.** Skincare brand → skincare publication. Forced relevance (skincare brand → general business publication writing on "leadership lessons from running a skincare brand") works occasionally but earns weaker authority signals.
- **Anchor strategy.** Guest posts give the brand control of the anchor. Use sparingly: 1-2 contextual links in the body, branded or naked-URL anchors most of the time, exact-match anchors rare. Over-optimising guest-post anchors is a manipulation signal.
- **Pitch angle.** Publications get hundreds of guest pitches; the angle has to be original, audience-relevant, and timely. "Why mineral sunscreen matters" is generic; "Three things sun-conscious skincare buyers get wrong about non-nano zinc oxide" is pitchable.
- **Contributor relationships.** Most quality placements come from cultivated relationships, not cold pitches. Editor LinkedIn relationships, conference encounters, prior journalist coverage, mutual contacts.
- **Measurement.** Guest posting yields are slow — placements take weeks to months from pitch to publish. Measure quarterly, not weekly.
- **The author byline matters.** A founder / dermatologist / expert byline is more credible than a generic "marketing team" byline. Whose name is on the piece materially affects acceptance rate at quality publications.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun has been doing some guest posting (3 placements in the past 12 months: 2 at small skincare blogs, 1 at a mid-tier publication). Yield per hour is unclear; the team wants to evaluate whether to scale, redesign, or deprioritise.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `plan-guest-posting-campaign`. Skill audits past placements, identifies target publications, designs pitch angles, recommends cadence.
>
> **Step 2.** Output:
>
> - **Past placement audit:** 3 placements; 1 produced ~30 referral visits + a quality follow-link, 2 produced negligible referral and one was actually on a content-mill (recommend disavow / monitor).
> - **Target publications (Tier 2-3):** Byrdie (DR 82, accepts contributor pitches via editor outreach), Real Simple (DR 80, strict editorial), Refinery29 (DR 81, very limited contributor slots), Insider Beauty (DR 88, mostly paid contributors), Sustainable Beauty (DR 45, accepts guest posts).
> - **Pitch angles (4):** "Three skin-tone-test findings most sunscreen reviews miss" (data-led, original); "What '20% non-nano zinc oxide' actually means on the bottle" (transparency-led); "How dermatologists evaluate mineral sunscreen safety" (expert-led, with co-byline); "Sustainable beauty without greenwashing claims" (voice-led).
> - **Cadence:** target 4-8 placements/quarter; 2-3 cold pitches + 1-2 cultivated relationships.
> - **Author strategy:** founder + dermatologist co-byline preferred; produces 2-3x acceptance rate vs. anonymous "marketing team."
>
> **Step 3.** Result: 6 months in, 7 placements landed (vs target 8-16), 2 of which became repeat-contributor relationships. Yield per hour is comparable to broken-link building; guest posting will continue at current cadence.

## How to do it

1. **Audit past placements** (if any). What was published where, what yield, what link quality.
2. **Identify target publications.** 10-20 quality publications relevant to the category. Mix of Tier 1 (premium, hard), Tier 2 (specialised, achievable), Tier 3 (niche, easier).
3. **Develop pitch angles.** 4-6 angles per quarter. Each is specific, original, audience-relevant. Avoid "why X matters" generics.
4. **Identify the right author.** Founder for some pieces; dermatologist / expert for credibility-sensitive pieces; senior team member for others. Author byline materially affects acceptance.
5. **Send personalised pitches.** Reference specific articles by the editor; tie the angle to recent coverage; keep the pitch short (3-5 paragraphs). Spam-volume pitches don't work.
6. **Cultivate relationships.** First placement is the hardest. After landing, follow up with a thank-you, a follow-on idea pitch within a month, and stay in touch with the editor.
7. **Track yield per hour.** Some publications are great links but cost 20+ hours of editing; others are quick. Track and rebalance.
8. **Avoid content-mill traps.** Sites that charge "review fees," publish anything, or have low editorial quality should be skipped regardless of DR.
9. **Run quarterly review.** Which publications worked, which didn't, what to scale or drop.

## Common pitfalls

- **Pitching content mills.** Sites that accept anything for a "review fee" are content mills. Even with technically high DR, the link profile signals manipulation.
- **Generic pitches.** "Hi, I'd love to contribute a guest post" gets ignored. Specific angle + tied to publication's recent coverage is the formula.
- **Wrong author.** "Field & Sun marketing team" gets fewer placements than "Sarah Chen, Founder, Field & Sun" or "Dr. Jane Smith, Board-Certified Dermatologist & Field & Sun Advisor."
- **Anchor-text over-optimisation.** Guest posts give the brand control of the anchor — the temptation is to use exact-match. Resist; mix branded / naked / partial.
- **No follow-up cadence.** First placement leads to 5-10 more if cultivated. Most teams ship one and disappear.
- **No publication-quality filter.** "We need links" → accepting any publication that says yes. The link profile suffers; some links are worse than no links.
- **Over-investing.** Guest posting is slow + medium-yield. Spending 50% of off-page time here is usually wrong; 15-25% is typical.
- **Ignoring topical relevance.** Skincare brand contributing to a "general business" publication earns a weaker signal than the same brand contributing to a skincare-relevant publication.
- **No measurement.** Without yield-per-hour data, guest posting becomes "we did some posts" reporting. Track and rebalance.
