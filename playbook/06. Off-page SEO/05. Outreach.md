# Outreach

> The operational discipline that powers everything else off-page. Email templates, prospecting, sender hygiene, follow-up cadence, CRM management. Without this layer, every other tactical channel underperforms.

## What it is

Outreach is the practice of identifying, contacting, following up with, and managing relationships with the people who can grant the brand visibility — journalists, editors, contributors, bloggers, podcasters, dermatologist advisors, partner sites. Tactical channels (digital PR, guest posting, broken-link, mention recovery) all *consume* outreach as a service. Outreach itself is the operational layer: who to contact, how to find them, what to send, how to follow up, how to track responses, how to maintain the relationships that compound over years.

For Field & Sun, outreach is not a tactic — it's the infrastructure. The same outreach process supports PR campaigns, guest-post pitches, mention-recovery, broken-link, HARO follow-ups. Brands that invest in outreach infrastructure (CRM, templates, sender warming, follow-up discipline) earn 3-5x the response rates of brands that don't, across every channel.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **response rate is the single biggest lever in off-page work**. A 1% response rate vs. 5% response rate is a 5x outcome difference for the same input hours. Outreach quality — sender reputation, personalisation, follow-up discipline — is what differentiates the two.

Second, **deliverability matters**. Cold outreach that goes to spam folders earns 0% reply rate. Sender-domain reputation, IP warming, SPF/DKIM/DMARC setup, send-volume discipline all affect inbox placement. Brands that send 200+ pitches/week from a single shared domain destroy their deliverability for every other purpose (sales, marketing, support).

Third, **relationships compound**. The first placement at a publication is the hardest; subsequent ones are dramatically easier. CRM-based relationship tracking (who's covered us, when, what next angle to send them) turns one-off pitches into a relationship portfolio. Brands that invest in relationship infrastructure win in 18-month windows; brands that don't, churn through cold pitching forever.

## Core concepts

- **Sender reputation.** Email-domain trustworthiness signal. Affected by: send volume, complaint rate, bounce rate, spam-trap hits, engagement rate. Low reputation = inbox-placement issues across all sends from that domain.
- **Sender warming.** Gradual ramp of send volume from new domains / IPs. Sending 1,000 emails on day 1 from a new domain triggers spam filters; ramping over 4-6 weeks does not.
- **Use a separate outreach domain.** Most brands use `outreach.brandname.com` or similar — keeps deliverability issues isolated from primary marketing domain. Properly authenticated (SPF/DKIM/DMARC).
- **Email infrastructure.** Pipl / Hunter / Apollo / Mailshake / Lemlist / Postaga / etc. for prospect-finding + sending. ESP choice matters less than discipline.
- **Templates as starting points, not finished pitches.** Templates produce the structure; personalisation produces response. "Hi [Name]" + paragraph 1 about a specific recent piece by them is the formula.
- **Follow-up cadence.** Most responses come from follow-ups, not initial pitch. 2-3 follow-ups across 10-14 days roughly doubles response rate.
- **CRM is the asset.** Spreadsheet (small scale) or actual CRM (Pipedrive, HubSpot, Streak, Close) tracking who you've contacted, when, what response, what next. Without CRM, the same person gets pitched the same angle from three different team members.
- **Quality > quantity.** Personalised pitches to 50 prospects often outperform templated pitches to 500. The math: 50 × 15% = 7.5 placements; 500 × 1% = 5 placements (with reputational damage from the volume).
- **Compliance.** GDPR / CAN-SPAM / PECR — outreach has legal requirements (unsubscribe link, identifying information, no scraping behind logins). Adhere.
- **Avoid sales-y voice.** Marketing / sales templates ("hope this finds you well!") perform worse than direct, journalist-respectful voice. Outreach templates should sound like a colleague, not a cold-caller.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun's marketing manager has been pitching journalists ad-hoc — different team members, different addresses, no CRM, generic templates. Response rates are around 1.5%. The team wants to professionalise.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `design-outreach-process`. Skill produces the operational blueprint covering domain / authentication setup, ESP choice, template library, CRM, follow-up cadence, and quality controls.
>
> **Step 2.** Output:
>
> - **Domain:** `fieldandsun.com` is primary; outreach uses dedicated subdomain `press.fieldandsun.com` (warmed over 4 weeks). SPF/DKIM/DMARC validated.
> - **Sender identity:** [Name] from `firstname@press.fieldandsun.com` (real human, not generic "press@" address; named sender outperforms generic 3-5x).
> - **ESP / tool:** Lemlist for sending + tracking; 100 sends / day cap; Apollo for prospect-finding; HubSpot CRM for relationship tracking.
> - **Template library:** 8 templates across 4 channels (PR pitch, guest-post pitch, mention-recovery, broken-link). Each template has 3-5 personalised variables.
> - **CRM schema:** journalist / editor name + publication + last contacted + last response + next-angle-to-pitch + relationship status.
> - **Follow-up cadence:** Day 0 / Day 4 / Day 10 / Day 14 (final). Three follow-ups; templates differ from initial.
> - **Quality controls:** every pitch reviewed before send (early-stage); response rate target 8-15%; deliverability monitored weekly.
>
> **Step 3.** Result: 90 days post-implementation, response rate climbs from 1.5% to 11% (7x improvement). Deliverability issues that previously affected primary domain transactional emails resolved (they were caused by ad-hoc outreach from primary domain).

## How to do it

1. **Audit current state.** What domain do outreach emails go from? Is it authenticated? What's response rate? What's deliverability? Is there a CRM?
2. **Set up outreach infrastructure.**
   - Dedicated subdomain (`press.brand.com` or similar).
   - SPF / DKIM / DMARC authentication.
   - Sender warming if new (4-6 weeks ramp).
   - ESP / sending tool (Lemlist / Mailshake / Postaga / etc.).
3. **Run `design-outreach-process`.** Produces the full operational blueprint.
4. **Build the template library.** 8-12 templates across PR, guest, mention-recovery, broken-link, HARO follow-up. Each with personalisation variables.
5. **Set up CRM.** Pipedrive, HubSpot, Streak (Gmail), or Close. Schema: contact + publication + history + next-step.
6. **Define follow-up cadence.** 3-4 follow-ups across 10-14 days, each with different content / angle.
7. **Define quality controls.** Pre-send review (especially early-stage); response-rate monitoring; deliverability monitoring.
8. **Train the team.** Anyone sending outreach uses the same templates + CRM + cadence.
9. **Iterate.** Templates that work get scaled; templates that don't get redesigned. CRM relationships get nurtured.
10. **Audit quarterly.** Sender reputation, deliverability, response rate, CRM hygiene.

## Common pitfalls

- **Sending from primary domain.** Cold outreach issues affect transactional / customer email deliverability. Use dedicated subdomain.
- **No authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC).** Half the sends go to spam. Free to set up; non-negotiable.
- **No sender warming on new domain.** New domain sending 100+ emails day 1 = blacklist. Ramp over 4-6 weeks.
- **Generic "press@" sender.** Named human sender ("Sarah from Field & Sun") outperforms 3-5x.
- **Templates without personalisation.** "Hi [Name], I'd love to..." spam pattern. Reference specific recent piece + personalise.
- **No follow-up.** 60-70% of placements come from follow-ups. Skipping them loses the majority of yield.
- **No CRM.** Same person pitched 3 times by different team members; relationships not tracked; angles not coordinated.
- **Sales voice.** "Hope this finds you well!" / "I wanted to reach out!" — sounds like cold sales. Direct + colleague-tone wins.
- **Volume over quality.** 500 templated pitches at 1% < 50 personalised at 15%. Volume + bad reputation compounds.
- **No compliance hygiene.** Missing unsubscribe / identifying info violates CAN-SPAM / GDPR. Legal risk + deliverability risk.
- **No quality control on early outreach.** Templates that ship before review can damage relationships. Pre-send review for first 30-60 days.
