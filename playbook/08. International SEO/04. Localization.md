# Localization

> The content layer of international SEO. Translation (machine vs human), cultural adaptation, currency / pricing, regulatory compliance per market, market-specific brand voice. Where international SEO becomes international UX.

## What it is

Localization is the practice of adapting content for a specific market beyond literal translation. It includes language translation (the obvious layer), cultural adaptation (imagery, examples, references, claims), currency / pricing / payment methods (market-appropriate), regulatory / legal compliance (GDPR, country-specific consumer-protection laws, FDA-equivalent in EU, etc.), and brand-voice nuances per market (the same brand can sound different in French vs German vs US English while remaining the same brand).

For Field & Sun, localization matters as soon as the brand expands beyond its home language-market. UK English → US English requires light localization (spelling, terminology, units). UK English → French requires full localization (translation, cultural adaptation, currency, regulatory). Each market has its own depth requirement.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **machine-translated content underperforms in 2026**. Google detects low-quality machine translation; AI Overviews and LLM citation surfaces favour authoritative, human-quality content. Brands relying solely on machine translation (DeepL, Google Translate, etc.) for production-ready content rank substantially below brands investing in human translation or quality-reviewed machine output.

Second, **localization is brand integrity in market**. A French customer reading a literal translation that sounds robotic, or a US-centric example that doesn't apply, or USD pricing on a French site experiences a brand that doesn't respect the market. Conversion rate reflects this directly — well-localized brands convert 2-3x better than literally-translated ones in non-home markets.

Third, **regulatory compliance is per-market**. EU markets require GDPR, Cookie Directive, EU Accessibility Act, country-specific consumer protection. US-only compliance won't survive an EU launch. Skipping legal localization is a launch-blocker that brands routinely under-budget.

## Core concepts

- **Translation methods.** Machine (Google Translate, DeepL — fastest, lowest cost, lowest quality). Machine + human review (faster than full human, quality acceptable). Human professional (translation agency, ~$0.10-0.30/word, highest quality). Native-speaker in-house (best for brand voice, slowest scale).
- **Translation memory.** Tools (Smartling, Crowdin, Lokalise, Phrase) that store translated phrases for reuse. Reduces cost over time + maintains consistency.
- **Cultural adaptation.** Beyond translation: examples, imagery, customer references, holidays mentioned, currency display, units, product positioning ("indoor swimming" vs "open-water swimming" relevance varies by climate).
- **Brand voice consistency.** A brand's voice can have local variation (more formal in German markets, more casual in Australian markets) without losing identity. Captured in per-market style guide.
- **Regulatory localization.** Cookie banners, privacy policy, terms of service, returns policy, shipping disclosures — all market-specific. Some markets require specific language / placement (EU Cookie Directive).
- **Currency + pricing.** Display in local currency; round to local conventions (€19.99 vs €20.00 differs by market norm); payment methods (iDEAL in NL, Klarna in SE, etc.).
- **Date + number formats.** US: 5/1/2026; EU: 1/5/2026 or 2026-05-01; "5,000.00" (US) vs "5.000,00" (EU). Format mismatches signal "not for this market."
- **SEO-specific localization.** Keywords don't translate literally; conduct per-market keyword research. UK "lift" vs US "elevator"; German "Sonnencreme" vs "Sonnenschutz" — different search volume, different intent nuance.
- **Localization is ongoing.** New content needs new localization; product launches need market-specific copy; regulatory changes per market need updates.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun is launching in France, Germany, Italy. The team has decided on subdirectory architecture (`fieldandsun.com/fr/`, `/de/`, `/it/`). Now they need the localization plan.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `plan-localisation`. Skill produces the per-market content adaptation plan covering translation method, cultural adaptation, regulatory, brand-voice considerations, SEO-specific keyword research per market.
>
> **Step 2.** Output:
>
> - **Translation method:** machine (DeepL) + human review by native-speaker freelancers per market. ~$8-15k upfront for full site translation; faster than full human-only.
> - **Cultural adaptation:** France: emphasis on dermatologist credibility (high in French skincare market); Germany: emphasis on ingredient transparency + technical specs (German consumer norm); Italy: emphasis on "Made in" / origin + family / tradition (Italian beauty norm).
> - **Regulatory:**
>   - France: language law (Toubon Law) requires French primary on French market.
>   - Germany: stricter consumer-protection (Verbraucherschutz); long-form returns policy.
>   - Italy: VAT display requirements; specific shipping disclosure language.
>   - All EU: GDPR, Cookie Directive, EU Accessibility Act (already required), CE marking implications.
> - **Brand voice:** more formal in German market; warmer in Italian; sophisticated in French. Per-market style guide drafted.
> - **Currency:** EUR display; €19.99 pricing convention.
> - **Date formats:** EU `dd/mm/yyyy`.
> - **Keyword research:** per-market — French "crème solaire minérale" (1,800/mo), German "mineralische Sonnencreme" (2,400/mo), Italian "crema solare minerale" (900/mo). Different volumes, different long-tail intent.
> - **SEO-specific localization:** title tags, meta descriptions, headers translated and locally optimised (not literal); URL slugs in local language (`/fr/guide-creme-solaire-minerale/`).
> - **Per-market PR / off-page:** local journalists + publications per market (handoff to Off-page).
>
> **Step 3.** Result: 4-month localization rollout (translation 2 months + regulatory 1 month + per-market keyword research + off-page setup ongoing). EU launch ships with proper localization; first-12-months EU revenue exceeds expected by 35% vs literal-translation baseline.

## How to do it

1. **Run `plan-localisation`** per market. Skill produces the localization plan covering translation method, cultural adaptation, regulatory, brand voice, currency, date formats, SEO keyword research.
2. **Decide translation method per market.** Match budget + scale + quality requirement.
3. **Build per-market style guide.** Brand voice in the local language, tone variations, banned terms, preferred terms.
4. **Per-market keyword research.** Don't translate keywords literally; conduct fresh research per market.
5. **Translate priority templates first.** Homepage, pillar, top PDPs, checkout flow. Then expand.
6. **Layer cultural adaptation.** Imagery, examples, references — per-market.
7. **Regulatory + legal review.** Per market. EU markets: GDPR, Cookie Directive, EU Accessibility Act, country-specific. Coordinate with legal counsel.
8. **Currency + pricing display.** Per-market checkout, payment methods, tax / VAT.
9. **Date + number format display.** Per-market display.
10. **Quality control.** Native-speaker review of priority templates before launch. Spot-check post-launch.
11. **Ongoing localization.** New content / product launches need per-market translation + adaptation. Translation memory reduces incremental cost.

## Common pitfalls

- **Machine translation as final.** Google and customers detect low quality; conversion rate reflects.
- **Literal translation.** "Free shipping" doesn't translate well to all markets; some require "shipping included" or other phrasing for legal / cultural reasons.
- **Skipping cultural adaptation.** Translation alone doesn't make a US page feel French; imagery + examples + references matter.
- **Currency display without pricing review.** Translating $19.99 to €19.99 ignores tax / VAT / market pricing norms.
- **Ignoring regulatory.** EU markets have substantial compliance; non-compliance = legal + brand risk.
- **No per-market keyword research.** Translating English keywords to French doesn't capture French search behaviour.
- **One translation tool, all markets.** Different markets benefit from different translation approaches.
- **No native-speaker review.** Machine + non-native review produces awkward content; native review catches what others miss.
- **Forgetting brand-voice variation.** German vs French vs Italian markets have different formality / tone norms; brand can vary while staying consistent.
- **No translation memory.** Without it, every new piece of content gets translated from scratch; cost compounds.
