# Descriptions

> The two-line snippet below the title in the SERP. Doesn't directly affect rankings — but heavily affects whether a searcher clicks, and clicks are a ranking signal.

## What it is

The meta description is the HTML `<meta name="description">` tag of a page. Google uses it as the snippet shown under the title in search results, alongside the URL. It's not a guaranteed display — Google rewrites about a third of meta descriptions when it thinks a snippet pulled from page content is more relevant — but on the two-thirds where Google honours your description, it's prime real estate for converting an impression into a click.

## Why it matters

The meta description doesn't directly influence which keywords you rank for. What it influences is **click-through rate (CTR)** — the percentage of people who see your result and click it. CTR matters in two ways: it converts impressions into traffic (which converts into revenue), and it feeds back into rankings (Google watches CTR by query and adjusts ranks accordingly over time).

For a D2C brand, the meta description is one of the cheapest CTR levers available. Rewriting descriptions on the top 20-50 pages typically takes a few hours, ships immediately, and produces measurable lift on positions 4-15 within 30 days. Pages already at #1 see less benefit (they're already getting the lion's share of clicks); pages at positions 5-15 see the most.

## Core concepts

- **Length and truncation** — Google truncates around 150-160 characters on desktop, ~120 on mobile. Aim for 140-155 characters so the full description shows on desktop and at minimum the first sentence shows on mobile.
- **Front-load value** — assume the second half might be cut off on mobile. The first ~120 characters should stand alone as a complete pitch.
- **Match the searcher's question** — the best descriptions reflect the *intent* of the query. For a commercial query, lead with the comparison or the proof. For a transactional query, lead with what's offered + the offer (free shipping, X% off, returns).
- **Action language** — descriptions read better with one direct call ("Shop X," "Learn how Y," "Compare Z") rather than passive description.
- **Brand voice presence** — descriptions are short enough that voice mismatches are jarring. Warm-and-conversational pages need warm-and-conversational descriptions; clinical-and-expert pages need that too.
- **Google rewrites** — if Google rewrites your description ~33% of the time on average, it usually means either (a) the page itself has more relevant phrasing for the query, or (b) the description is generic. Tighten the description toward the actual cluster intent and the rewrite rate drops.
- **Keyword inclusion (with care)** — including the primary keyword in the description doesn't directly help rankings, but Google bolds matching query terms in the snippet, which lifts CTR. Include it naturally; don't stuff.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** The same `/products/daily-glow-serum` page from the [Title example](01.%20Title.md). After title rewrite it's at position 5. Current meta description is auto-generated from the first paragraph: "Welcome to our serum collection. Shop our range of hydrating serum products including daily glow..." (155 chars, generic, no proof points).
>
> **Step 1.** Pull intent and cluster. Transactional, primary "hydrating serum." The SERP at #5 surrounds them with brand product pages and prestige-skin listicles.
>
> **Step 2.** Generate variants:
>
> - "Hydrating serum for radiance — 15% vitamin C, 4% niacinamide, and hyaluronic acid in a refillable routine." (119 chars)
> - "Hydrating serum for dull, uneven-looking skin. Ingredient-led daily formula with vitamin C, niacinamide, and hyaluronic acid." (132 chars)
> - "Shop Daily Glow Serum — lightweight hydration, vitamin C, and niacinamide for a simple morning skincare routine." (118 chars)
>
> **Step 3.** Score each: length within target, primary keyword present, intent fit (commercial proof + transactional offer), voice match, distinct call to action, freshness vs. SERP neighbours.
>
> **Step 4.** Pick variant 1 — leads with the benefit (radiance), includes the offer (fast shipping), and matches a confident-warm brand voice.
>
> **Step 5.** Implement, request indexing, monitor CTR. After 30 days CTR is up from 4.1% to 5.4% — additional ~28 monthly clicks at the same position.

## How to do it

1. **Pull from the same priority list as titles.** Descriptions and titles ship together for the same set of pages — both are SERP-facing CTR levers. Don't optimise descriptions independently of titles.
2. **Read the cluster + intent.** The description must answer the searcher's intent in their language. A transactional searcher wants product proof + offer; an informational searcher wants confidence the page will explain.
3. **Look at the SERP description patterns.** Many SERPs have an unofficial "shape" — pages at top positions tend to lead with similar proof points. Match the shape, differentiate on substance.
4. **Draft 3-4 variants from different angles.** Differentiator-led (what makes this page distinct), benefit-led (the customer outcome), offer-led (what the user gets if they click), proof-led (credentials, scale, reviews).
5. **Score for length, keyword presence, intent fit, voice, and call-to-action.** Use the `generate-meta-descriptions` skill to do this consistently.
6. **Pick one and implement.** Update the description in your CMS, request indexing in GSC.
7. **Monitor CTR for 30 days.** Watch the GSC queries report. If CTR didn't move (and position stayed steady), the description is fine but not winning — try a different angle next round. If CTR dropped, revert.
8. **Re-evaluate any time Google starts rewriting.** GSC won't tell you Google is rewriting, but you can spot it: search the URL's primary query, see what's actually shown, compare to your description. If they don't match, the description isn't doing its job — the rewrite is your CTR ceiling.

## Common pitfalls

- **Auto-generated descriptions.** Most CMSs default to "use the first X characters of the page" if the meta description is empty. That's almost always worse than a deliberate one — the first paragraph of a product page is usually a generic intro, not a snippet that wins clicks.
- **Stuffing the keyword.** Two or three repetitions of the primary keyword in 150 characters reads as spam to humans and doesn't help Google. One natural inclusion is enough.
- **Length panic.** "Exactly 155 characters" is not the goal. 140-155 with a clean cut after the first sentence on mobile is the goal. A 130-character description that's tight beats a 158-character one that's padded.
- **Generic copy.** "Welcome to our store" / "Shop our wide range" / "Learn more about our products." These don't earn clicks. Specificity wins.
- **Forgetting mobile.** Mobile cuts at ~120 characters. If your call to action is in characters 130-155, half your traffic will never see it.
- **Misaligning with the page.** A description promising "Free shipping over $40" on a page that doesn't offer that creates a bounce. The promise has to match what's on the page.
- **Treating descriptions as set-and-forget.** Like titles, descriptions are A/B-testable. The first version is rarely the winner. Iterate on top pages every 6-12 months.
