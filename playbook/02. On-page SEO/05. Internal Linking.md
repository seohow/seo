# Internal Linking

> The wiring of your site. How pages link to each other determines how Google understands topical relationships, where ranking signal flows, and which pages users (and crawlers) discover.

## What it is

Internal links are hyperlinks from one page on your site to another page on the same site. Different from external links (which point to other domains) and backlinks (which point at you from other domains). Internal linking covers the navigation menu, footer, breadcrumbs, contextual in-body links, related-product widgets, and "you might also like" modules.

The pattern that emerges from your internal links is your **site architecture** — the implicit map of which pages are most important, which topics group together, and how Google should distribute the ranking equity flowing in from external links.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, internal links **distribute PageRank** (link equity). Pages that earn external links pass some equity onward via their internal links. A homepage with strong external authority but no internal links to deeper pages wastes that authority on itself. Smart internal linking spreads the equity to the pages that need it.

Second, internal links **signal topical relationships**. Google reads the cluster of pages mutually linking to each other as a coherent topical unit — exactly the pattern modern Google rewards. Sites with strong topical depth (10 interlinked posts on related sub-topics) outrank sites with isolated pages on the same primary keyword.

Third, internal links **help discovery and crawl efficiency**. Pages that are 5+ clicks from the homepage get crawled less often and rank worse on average. Pages buried with no internal links pointing at them are basically invisible. A page is only as crawlable as its internal links make it.

For a D2C brand, internal linking is most often under-invested — the navigation and footer are taken seriously, but contextual in-body links and related-content modules are afterthoughts. Fixing this is a free uplift on traffic and rankings across a lot of pages, with no new content required.

## Core concepts

- **Anchor text** — the clickable text of an internal link. Anchor text is a strong topic signal. "Click here" is wasted; "tinted mineral sunscreen" passes the topical signal to the linked page.
- **Topical clusters / hubs** — a cluster of pages on related sub-topics, all linking to and from a central pillar page. The pattern Google rewards. From your `clusters/cluster-map.md`, every cluster has natural internal-linking implications.
- **Pillar / hub pages** — broad pages that link out to many specific pages on related sub-topics. The "spoke" pages link back to the hub. Hub-and-spoke is the canonical structure for topical depth.
- **Click depth** — number of clicks from the homepage to a page. Shallower is better; pages 1-3 clicks deep get crawled and ranked best. Pages 5+ clicks deep often need direct internal links from high-authority pages to compensate.
- **Equity flow** — link equity flows roughly proportionally to position. A header-nav link from every page is enormous; a single in-body link from one page is small. Balance: header nav for navigationally-important pages; contextual in-body links for topically-related pages.
- **Orphan pages** — pages with zero internal links pointing at them. They exist (in the sitemap, in the CMS), but they're effectively invisible to Google. The first audit deliverable on most sites is the orphan-page list.
- **Internal anchor diversity** — Google looks at the variety of anchor texts pointing to a page. If 100 internal links all use "tinted mineral sunscreen" as anchor, Google reads it as suspicious; some natural variation is expected.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** The D2C brand has 28 clusters in `clusters/cluster-map.md`. The site has 6 product pages, 5 collection pages, 22 blog posts, plus the homepage and FAQ. After running [Title](01.%20Title.md) and [Header Structure](03.%20Header%20Structure.md) work, on-page is in shape. Internal linking is next.
>
> **Step 1.** Audit. Crawl the site (Screaming Frog). Identify: 4 orphan blog posts with zero inbound internal links; 3 collection pages reachable only via the main nav (no contextual links); inconsistent anchor text on the highest-traffic blog post (anchor "click here" on 60% of links).
>
> **Step 2.** Plan. From the cluster map, identify the 3 highest-leverage hub topics (sun care, serums/skincare, refills). For each hub, design hub-and-spoke linking:
>
> - Pillar = collection page (or new pillar blog post if collection is too commercial).
> - Spokes = the 4-7 cluster pages that fit the hub topic.
> - Spokes link to pillar; pillar links to all spokes; spokes link to 2-3 sibling spokes contextually.
>
> **Step 3.** Add linking matrix:
>
> - Homepage → 3 pillars (already in nav).
> - Each pillar → its 4-7 spokes (in body content + a "Related" module).
> - Each spoke → its pillar (in intro + body) + 2-3 sibling spokes (contextual).
> - Orphan blog posts → either link them in from related spokes (if topically fit) or de-publish them.
>
> **Step 4.** Implement. CMS work to add the related modules; manual contextual linking on the priority cluster pages.
>
> **Step 5.** After 60 days: 4 of 5 collection pages rank ↑, 14 of 22 blog posts rank ↑ for cluster keywords, no orphan pages remaining. Total cost: ~12 hours of work. Compounding traffic gain across the next 6 months.

## How to do it

1. **Pull the cluster map.** Internal linking strategy starts from `clusters/cluster-map.md`. Each cluster maps to one page; clusters that group together topically are candidates for hub-and-spoke linking.
2. **Audit current state.** Crawl the site (Screaming Frog, Sitebulb, Ahrefs Site Audit). Identify: orphan pages, click depth distribution, anchor-text consistency, redirect chains in internal links, broken internal links.
3. **Design the hub structure.** For 3-7 priority topical hubs, identify the pillar page and the 4-10 spoke pages. Sketch the linking matrix.
4. **Audit anchor-text quality.** For each high-priority page, check what anchor texts point at it from inside the site. If they're all "click here" or all the same exact-match keyword, fix.
5. **Plan additions, not just fixes.** Where should new internal links *be added*? On every cluster spoke, there should be 2-3 contextual links to sibling spokes (not just "Related products" widgets — actual in-body sentences).
6. **Plan removals.** Internal links to retired pages, orphaned URLs, redirect chains, or low-value pages dilute equity. Identify and clean.
7. **Implement in batches.** CMS-level changes (related modules, footer, breadcrumbs) ship together. Per-page contextual additions ship in batches by cluster — start with the highest-priority hub and work down.
8. **Monitor.** After 60 days, re-crawl. Look at: orphan-page count (should be 0 or near-zero), click-depth distribution (improved), anchor-text diversity for top pages (broader), rank changes on linked pages.

## Common pitfalls

- **Treating internal linking as a footer/nav job only.** The footer and nav are the *least* equity-passing internal links because they're on every page (Google compresses repeat patterns). The highest-leverage internal links are contextual, in-body links from topically-related pages.
- **"Click here" and generic anchor text.** Wastes the strongest topical signal you can give Google for free.
- **Exact-match anchor stuffing.** The opposite mistake. 100 internal links all using the exact same keyword anchor reads as manipulative. Aim for natural variation.
- **Orphan pages.** The single most fixable internal-linking mistake. Every page should have at least 1-2 inbound internal links.
- **Hub-and-spoke without the spokes linking back to the hub.** Half the pattern doesn't work. Both directions are required.
- **Redirect chains in internal links.** Every internal link should point to a 200-status URL, not a redirect. Update internal links whenever URLs change.
- **Linking from low-authority pages to your highest-priority pages.** Internal-linking equity flows from high-authority pages outward. The link from your homepage is worth more than the link from a 2-year-old blog post with no traffic.
- **Adding "Related Products" / "You Might Also Like" modules and stopping there.** These help, but they're auto-generated and use template anchors. Manual contextual links from in-body text are the high-leverage version.
