# SEO Experiments

> Running structured experiments to learn what actually moves rankings, citation rate, and feature presence — rather than relying on conventional wisdom or single-anecdote case studies. The learning system that turns each quarter's investment into the next quarter's playbook.

## What it is

SEO experimentation is the discipline of running pre-registered, statistically-rigorous tests on SEO interventions to learn what works for your specific brand, vertical, and content shape. Common experiment types: title rewrites at scale, internal-linking changes, schema additions / removals, content-pattern interventions (TL;DR addition; question-led H2 reframes; freshness signal additions), URL structure changes, FAQ additions, image-pack treatments. The output of an experiment is a learning that updates briefs, standards, or strategy — not just a single page's outcome.

The crucial distinction from related topics: **A/B Testing** (UX leaf 04) is conversion-focused — does this checkout button, headline, or hero image improve conversion? **SEO Experiments** (this leaf) is ranking / citation / feature-focused — does this title pattern improve ranking, does this schema addition lift AI Overview citation, does this internal-link change move a cluster's traffic? Same underlying methodology (hypothesis, variants, sample, measurement, statistical guardrails); different outcome metrics; different SEO-safety considerations.

For Field & Sun, the question this leaf answers is: "we have the operational basics in place; we ship 12 posts/month; we run topical-authority work — but how do we know which of our SEO interventions actually move the needle?" The team has hunches: the founder believes question-led H2s lift AI citation; the marketing lead suspects FAQ schema lifts mobile CTR more than desktop. Without structured experiments, hunches stay hunches. With 4-6 well-designed experiments per year, the team converts hunches to learnings, learnings to brief-template upgrades, and brief-template upgrades to compounding quality. The cumulative effect over 24 months is a brief / standards / playbook that's specifically tuned to *this brand's* outcomes — not generic SEO best-practice — which is the moat.

## Why it matters

Three reasons. First, **most SEO advice is anecdote**. "Add FAQ schema and CTR goes up" is true sometimes, false other times, and neutral in between depending on vertical, page type, query intent, and SERP layout. Without your own experiments, you operate on someone else's anecdote — usually ill-fitted to your specific situation.

Second, **experiment learnings compound**. A learning from Q1 becomes a brief-template change in Q2 that compounds across every piece shipped from Q2 onwards. Two well-designed experiments per year, accumulated over four years, produce a brand-specific playbook that's hard for competitors to replicate without similar investment.

Third, **experiments protect against false positives**. SEO is full of "I changed X and ranking went up" stories. Many of those are noise — algorithm updates, competitor changes, seasonal patterns, regression to the mean. Pre-registered experiments with statistical guardrails distinguish signal from noise.

## Core concepts

- **Pre-registration.** Before launching the experiment, lock the hypothesis, variant definitions, sample / scope, success criteria, and statistical guardrails *in writing*. Pre-registration prevents post-hoc rationalisation ("it didn't move ranking, but engagement metrics improved!"). The experiment passes or fails against pre-registered criteria.
- **Hypothesis specificity.** "Adding FAQ schema improves CTR" is too vague. "Adding FAQ schema to mobile-priority blog posts (n=20 pages) lifts mobile CTR by ≥5% over 60 days, holding desktop CTR flat or improved" is testable.
- **Variant design.** SEO experiments are most often *split-pages* (some pages get treatment; matched control pages don't) rather than split-traffic (which is what UX A/B testing usually does). Split-pages requires careful matching: similar traffic, similar query intent, similar content type, similar age.
- **Sample size and statistical power.** With 5 pages getting treatment, a small difference is unmeasurable. Sample size depends on baseline metric variance and effect size you want to detect. For ranking changes: 20+ pages per arm typical for meaningful detection; for CTR changes on already-ranking pages: 10-20 typical; for cluster-level traffic: weeks of post-treatment data.
- **Duration.** SEO experiments need 30-90 days post-launch for crawl + re-evaluation + ranking stabilisation. UX A/B testing measures days; SEO experiments measure weeks-to-months.
- **A/A check.** Before running an A/B (treatment vs control), run an A/A (control vs control) for a few weeks to establish baseline variance. If A/A shows large differences, the matching is poor or the metric is too noisy.
- **SEO safety review.** A test that breaks ranking on the treatment pages is not safe to run. Pre-launch SEO-safety review checks: no canonical / indexing changes; no URL changes; no de-indexing; no critical-page treatment without rollback plan. UX leaf 04 covers the same discipline for A/B tests.
- **Confounders.** Algorithm updates, competitor moves, seasonal patterns, related content launches — all confound SEO experiments. Track confounders explicitly; flag any that fall during the test window; consider extending or invalidating.
- **Null results count.** Most SEO experiments produce null results. That's a learning — the intervention didn't move the metric in this brand / vertical / context. Null results update the brief / standards by *not* adding the pattern. Treat them as wins.
- **Experiment cadence.** 1-2 well-designed experiments per quarter is sustainable. More than 4 per quarter risks confounding (multiple changes simultaneously). Less than 1 per quarter is a learning-rate plateau.
- **Experiment portfolio.** Mix of small (per-page intervention; 30-day window), medium (cluster-level; 60-day window), and large (site-wide template change; 90+-day window). Different effort, different leverage.
- **Learnings → standards.** Every experiment outcome (positive, negative, null) updates *something*: brief template, technical SEO checklist, voice rubric, schema spec, or strategy. If a learning doesn't update an artifact, it doesn't compound.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun's founder believes that adding a TL;DR block to long-form blog posts lifts AI citation rate (per AI SEO leaf 01 patterns). Marketing lead is sceptical — "we already saw a CTR lift with FAQ schema; isn't that the priority?" The team has time for one experiment in Q3.
>
> **Step 1.** Run `design-seo-experiment` to pick which experiment to run + design it.
>
> - **Candidate 1: TL;DR addition on long-form blog posts.** Hypothesis: adding TL;DR to long-form blog posts (>1,500 words) lifts AI Overview / Perplexity citation rate. Effort: ~30 min/page × 20 pages = 10 hr.
> - **Candidate 2: Question-led H2 rewrites at scale.** Hypothesis: reframing topic-label H2s as question-led H2s lifts featured snippet capture rate. Effort: ~45 min/page × 15 pages = 11 hr.
> - **Candidate 3: FAQ schema retrospective.** Hypothesis: confirming whether the existing FAQ schema rollout (10 PDPs) actually lifted mobile CTR vs control PDPs. No new effort; analysis only.
> - **Pick:** Candidate 3 (FAQ schema retrospective) first — already-shipped experiment with data; result will inform whether to expand. Then Candidate 1 in Q4.
>
> **Step 2.** Design the FAQ schema retrospective:
>
> - **Hypothesis:** PDPs with FAQ schema (n=10, treated April 2026) have higher mobile CTR than matched control PDPs (n=10, no FAQ schema) over 60 days post-treatment.
> - **Pre-registered success criterion:** treated PDPs show ≥5% mobile CTR lift over matched controls in the 60-day window.
> - **Pre-registered null criterion:** treated PDPs show ≤2% lift (or any decline). Result is null; pattern does not get added to PDP template.
> - **Variants:** treatment = PDPs with FAQ schema; control = matched PDPs without. Matching: similar product category, traffic volume, page age, target query intent.
> - **Sample:** 10 vs 10 PDPs.
> - **Duration:** retrospective covers May–June 2026 (60 days post-treatment).
> - **Confounders to check:** product launches (none in window); seasonal (sun care peaks in May–August — control set must be sun-care-balanced); algorithm updates (Google March-2026 update preceded; should be settled).
> - **A/A reference:** prior 60-day window (March-April 2026) before any FAQ schema added — variance baseline.
> - **SEO-safety review:** schema only added (no canonical / index / URL changes); rollback plan: schema can be removed; no critical-page-only treatment.
>
> **Step 3.** Run the analysis:
>
> - Treated PDPs mobile CTR: 4.2% (May 2026 → 4.7% (June 2026 average).
> - Control PDPs mobile CTR: 4.1% → 4.3%.
> - A/A reference variance: ±0.3 percentage points.
> - Lift: treated +0.6 / +0.5 over control vs A/A variance ±0.3 → significant.
> - Mobile-only check: desktop CTR essentially flat across both arms.
>
> **Step 4.** Result + learning:
>
> - **Pre-registered success criterion met:** ≥5% mobile CTR lift achieved (4.2% → 4.7% = ~12% relative; control ~5%; net +~7%).
> - **Learning:** FAQ schema lifts mobile CTR meaningfully on PDPs (in our context); does not affect desktop CTR.
> - **Standards update:** PDP template gets FAQ schema by default *where genuine FAQ content exists*; no schema theatre — match the AI SEO leaf 01 + leaf 04 discipline.
> - **Brief update:** PDP brief now includes FAQ section requirement (minimum 4 questions) for any new product launch.
> - **Cross-category implications:** AI SEO leaf 04 (SERP Features) noted that FAQ rich result is reachable; this experiment validates the implementation. Topical-authority assessment can update the depth dimension (PDPs now have richer schema + content).
>
> **Step 5.** Document the experiment outcome in `businesses/field-and-sun/growth/seo-experiment-faq-schema-retrospective-2026-Q3.md`. Update PDP brief template + technical SEO checklist. Pre-register Q4 experiment (Candidate 1 — TL;DR on long-form).

## How to do it

1. **Pick experiments by leverage.** What learning would change the most downstream work? Cluster-level interventions > per-page interventions; standards-changing experiments > one-off tests.
2. **Pre-register everything.** Hypothesis, variants, sample, success / null criteria, confounders, SEO-safety check — locked in writing before launch.
3. **Match sample carefully.** SEO experiments live or die on sample matching. Treatment and control must be similar in traffic volume, intent, content type, page age, and competitive context.
4. **Run an A/A check first.** Establishes baseline variance. If A/A variance is too high, the experiment is unmeasurable and needs different metrics or larger sample.
5. **Stick to the duration.** Don't peek mid-test and decide based on early data. SEO experiments need 30-90 days post-launch.
6. **Review SEO safety pre-launch.** Treatment that breaks ranking on test pages is not safe. Cross-reference UX leaf 04 SEO-safety patterns.
7. **Track confounders explicitly.** Algorithm updates, competitor changes, seasonal patterns, content launches in the cluster. Confounders during the window may invalidate the test.
8. **Accept null results.** Most experiments don't produce significant effects. Null is a learning; document it; don't post-hoc rationalise.
9. **Update standards from learnings.** Every experiment outcome updates *something* — brief template, technical SEO checklist, voice rubric, strategy doc. If no artifact updates, the experiment didn't compound.
10. **Cadence: 1-2 per quarter.** Sustainable; avoids confound; matches measurement cycles.

## Common pitfalls

- **No pre-registration.** Post-hoc rationalisation is the most-common failure. Lock the criteria before launch.
- **Sample too small.** 5-page experiments rarely produce significant effects on noisy metrics. Plan sample size against expected effect size.
- **Mismatched treatment / control.** The control PDPs are a different product category, much higher traffic, or much older — invalidating the comparison. Spend time on matching.
- **Peeking and deciding mid-test.** Early data often flips later; deciding early produces false positives.
- **Ignoring confounders.** Algorithm update during the window? Cluster-level content launch? Competitor move? Track and decide whether to invalidate.
- **No A/A check.** Without baseline variance, you don't know what counts as significant.
- **Confusing UX A/B testing infrastructure with SEO experimentation.** UX A/B (split-traffic) is rarely appropriate for SEO; split-pages with matched controls is the right shape.
- **Treatment that breaks ranking.** Pre-launch SEO-safety review is non-negotiable.
- **Null result re-framed as success.** "It didn't move CTR but engagement metrics improved!" is post-hoc rationalisation. Honour the pre-registration.
- **No artifact update from learning.** Experiment shipped, results captured, no brief / standards / strategy doc updated. The learning evaporates.
- **Too many simultaneous experiments.** Multiple changes overlap; confound; can't isolate effects. 1-2 per quarter is the right cadence.
- **Generic experiment selection.** Running tests because "everyone runs FAQ schema tests" rather than because the learning matters for *this brand*. Pick experiments based on what would change downstream work.
