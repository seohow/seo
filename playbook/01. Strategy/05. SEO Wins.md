# SEO Wins

> Surface and prioritise the highest-leverage SEO opportunities you can act on this quarter — so research turns into a backlog, not a binder.

## What it is

The SEO Wins backlog is the working list of opportunities your strategy work has surfaced, scored and sequenced so the team always knows what to do next. It draws inputs from keyword research, intent classification, competitor analysis, and clustering — and it answers the only question that matters once the strategy phase ends: *what do we do first?*

Wins fall into a few archetypes: easy ranking improvements (pages already on page 2 that need a nudge), content gaps with high commercial value, technical fixes blocking crawl or indexing, on-page misses that are cheap to correct, and link opportunities ready to pursue. Each is scored on impact, effort, and confidence so the highest-ROI items rise to the top.

## Why it matters

Most SEO strategies die in the gap between research and execution. Founders end up with a 70-tab spreadsheet and no clear next step. The wins backlog closes that gap. It's the difference between "we did SEO research" and "we shipped 8 wins this quarter that grew organic 40%." For a D2C brand operating with finite hours, the discipline of "score, sequence, ship" is the multiplier on every other piece of work in this category.

The framing also matters strategically: organic SEO is compounding, but only after you ship. A backlog that ships 1-2 wins per week beats a strategy doc that ships nothing.

## Core concepts

- **The wins archetype map** — six common types: striking-distance keywords (already ranking on page 2-3), content-gap wins (a competitor ranks, you don't), on-page wins (cheap fixes to live pages), technical wins (crawl/index/speed blockers), refresh wins (existing pages that have decayed), and link wins (outreach-ready opportunities). Different archetypes go to different teams and have different effort profiles.
- **ICE / RICE scoring** — frameworks that quantify priority. ICE = Impact × Confidence × Ease. RICE = Reach × Impact × Confidence / Effort. Either is fine; pick one and stick with it. Subjective scoring is OK — the goal is comparing items against each other, not absolute precision.
- **Striking-distance opportunity** — a page already ranking 11-30 for a relevant keyword is the highest-confidence type of win. Nudging it to page 1 typically takes hours, not weeks.
- **Definition of done per win** — every win needs an explicit definition of "done" or it becomes scope creep. "Update the title tag" is done; "make the page better" never is.
- **Velocity vs deep work** — a healthy backlog mixes quick wins (ship this week) with strategic projects (ship this quarter). Too many quick wins = missed compounding bets; too many deep projects = no momentum.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** Field & Sun (D2C beauty brand) has finished keyword research, intent classification, competitor analysis, and clustering. They're sitting on ~32 cluster decisions and a competitor opportunity list of ~15 items. They need to know what to ship in the next 90 days.
>
> **Step 1.** They consolidate inputs into one backlog. Each input becomes one or more rows. Competitor opportunity #1 ("build [ingredient] science cluster") becomes 4 rows (4 spoke posts). Cluster S01 ("Mineral sunscreen collection") becomes 1 row.
>
> **Step 2.** They tag each row with archetype: 12 striking-distance, 18 content gap, 9 on-page, 4 technical, 6 refresh, 5 link.
>
> **Step 3.** They score each on Impact (1-5), Effort (1-5, lower = less effort), and Confidence (1-5). Compute Score = Impact × Confidence / Effort.
>
> **Step 4.** They sort and review. The top 10 rows include 4 striking-distance keywords (cheap title/H1 updates), 3 on-page wins (schema markup additions), 1 collection-page revamp, and 2 informational posts targeting commercial-adjacent gaps.
>
> **Step 5.** They sequence into 90 days: weeks 1-2 ship the 4 striking-distance updates plus 3 on-page fixes (~7 wins). Weeks 3-6 build the collection-page revamp and 2 informational posts. Weeks 7-12 tackle the [ingredient] science cluster and one technical item. The result is a sequenced quarter, not a wishlist.

## How to do it

1. **Consolidate the inputs.** Pull every opportunity surfaced by your strategy work into one place: cluster map, competitor opportunities, GSC striking-distance export, technical audit findings, refresh candidates, link prospects. The backlog is the *only* working artifact downstream — keep it definitive.
2. **Tag by archetype.** Striking-distance / content-gap / on-page / technical / refresh / link. The archetype tells you who owns the work and roughly how big it is.
3. **Score each item.** Pick ICE or RICE and apply consistently. Don't agonise over precision — directional comparisons are what matters.
4. **Define "done" for each win.** A specific, observable change. "Title tag updated to '[new]', published, indexed within 7 days." "Internal links added from 5 source pages." "FAQ schema added and validated in Rich Results Test." Vague wins become zombies.
5. **Sequence into time horizons.** Rough buckets: this week (quick wins), this month (mid-effort), this quarter (deep work). Aim for a weekly velocity floor (e.g. ship at least 2 wins per week) so momentum doesn't die.
6. **Assign ownership.** Even on a one-person team, write a name. Unowned items don't get done.
7. **Track outcomes, not just shipped.** For each shipped win, log what changed in rankings / traffic / clicks 30-60 days later. Your scoring will improve as you build evidence about what actually works for *your* domain.
8. **Keep the backlog alive.** Review weekly: ship from the top, add new items as you find them, prune items that have lost relevance, re-score items where evidence has shifted.

## Common pitfalls

- **Building a backlog and never shipping.** The single biggest failure mode. The backlog is a means; shipping is the end. If the backlog isn't shrinking, something is wrong.
- **Treating high-effort, high-impact items as P0.** They're often P1 — they shouldn't block weekly velocity. Quick wins ship while deep work is in progress.
- **Scoring optimistically across the board.** If everything is Impact 5, the prioritisation collapses. Be honest. Most items are Impact 2-3.
- **Confusing activity with output.** "Wrote a brief" is not a win. "Published page X targeting keyword Y, indexed within 7 days" is.
- **Ignoring confidence.** A brilliant idea you have low confidence in should be deprioritised vs. a smaller idea you have high confidence in. Confidence weighting is what prevents speculative work from crowding out compounding work.
- **One-and-done planning.** SEO work is iterative. The backlog has to be a live document — review weekly or it goes stale within a month.
