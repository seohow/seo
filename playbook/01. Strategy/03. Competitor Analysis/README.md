# Competitor Analysis

> Reverse-engineer who's already winning the SERPs you want, what they're doing well, and where the gaps are you can credibly fill.

## What it is

SEO competitor analysis is the structured study of the sites already ranking for the keywords you care about. The goal isn't to copy them — it's to understand the *pattern* of why they rank, identify the gaps they're not serving, and surface tactical opportunities you can actually execute against.

Two things to note: your **SEO competitors** are not always your **business competitors**. A D2C beauty brand competes against Sephora and Ulta for shelf space, but in SEO it competes against The Strategist, Byrdie, NYT Wirecutter, and Reddit threads. Both lists matter, for different reasons.

## Why it matters

The SERP is a finished market study, free, available in five seconds. Whoever ranks page 1 has been validated by Google as serving the query well. Studying them tells you the format that works, the depth required, the tone Google rewards, and the angles you'd otherwise have to discover by trial and error. Skipping competitor analysis is choosing to learn the same lessons more slowly and more expensively.

For a D2C brand, this is also where you find the highest-leverage gap opportunities — keywords your competitors rank for and you don't, content angles nobody is serving well, and link strategies that earned competitors their authority.

## Core concepts

- **SEO competitor vs. business competitor** — the two often diverge. Your SEO competitor is anyone ranking for your target keywords, whether they sell what you sell or not. Editorial publishers, Reddit threads, and YouTube videos can all be SEO competitors.
- **Keyword gap** — keywords your competitors rank for and you don't. The fastest source of opportunity for a domain that's been live a while.
- **Content gap** — questions, intents, or angles your competitors *don't* serve well. Harder to find but more defensible — you're not joining a crowded SERP, you're claiming an empty one.
- **Link gap** — domains linking to multiple competitors but not to you. A target list for outreach.
- **SERP overlap** — the percentage of keyword universe shared between you and a competitor. High overlap means it's a real SEO competitor; low overlap means they're an aspirational reference, not a head-to-head.
- **Topical authority signals** — how deeply a competitor covers a topic (volume of pages, internal linking density, breadth of subtopics). Tells you whether your strategy needs to match their depth or pick a more focused niche.

## A worked example

> **Scenario:** The D2C beauty brand wants to understand the SERP they're entering for "best mineral sunscreen" and adjacent skincare terms.
>
> **Step 1.** They list five competitors. Two direct (Supergoop, Blue Tansy), one aspirational (Drunk Elephant — bigger brand, similar positioning), one editorial (Wirecutter), one community (Reddit r/SkincareAddiction).
>
> **Step 2.** They run an organic keyword overlap report in Ahrefs/Semrush. Supergoop and Blue Tansy share ~45% of their keyword universe with the brand's target list. Drunk Elephant shares 30%. Wirecutter shares 55% — they rank for almost everything commercial in this space.
>
> **Step 3.** They run a content-gap report on Supergoop. They find 950 keywords Supergoop ranks for that the brand doesn't. After filtering for relevance, ~170 are actionable. Top opportunities: "[ingredient] in sunscreen" explainers, "does mineral sunscreen leave white cast" FAQ, "mineral sunscreen vs chemical" comparison.
>
> **Step 4.** They study the Wirecutter article that ranks #1 for "best sunscreen." 5,200 words, comparison table, ingredient analysis, photos of products, dermatologist sources, regularly updated. The pattern is clear: editorial-grade depth and expert credibility win this SERP.
>
> **Step 5.** They examine Supergoop's link profile. Top referring domains include beauty blogs, dermatology publications, and lifestyle roundups. They build a target list of 35 publications likely to also link to their brand if pitched correctly with a skincare angle.
>
> **Step 6.** Output: a 5-page competitor analysis doc with keyword gap, content gap, link gap, SERP-pattern observations, and prioritised opportunities.

## How to do it

1. **Define the competitor set.** 3-5 competitors is the sweet spot. Mix: 2-3 direct (same product), 1 aspirational (bigger version of you, similar values), 1 editorial (publisher ranking for your topics), and optionally 1 community (Reddit, niche forum). Be specific — a list of 20 dilutes the analysis.
2. **Confirm SERP overlap.** Use a tool's "competing domains" or "overlap" report to validate that the competitors you picked are actually competing in search. If overlap is below ~10%, they're a brand competitor, not an SEO competitor — note them but de-prioritise.
3. **Run the keyword gap.** For each direct competitor, identify keywords they rank for (typically positions 1-20) that you don't. Filter by relevance to your business and by realistic difficulty for your domain rating.
4. **Run the content gap.** Look at their top pages by organic traffic. What page types win for them? Are there topics, angles, or formats they don't serve well? This is where you find the defensible openings.
5. **Study 2-3 of the highest-traffic SERPs in detail.** For each, look at positions 1-5: page type, word count, page structure (H2s, table of contents, comparison tables), media, freshness, internal links, and the schema markup. The pattern reveals what Google rewards on this query type.
6. **Run a link gap.** Use Ahrefs/Semrush's link-intersect feature: domains linking to two or more competitors but not to you. These are your highest-probability outreach targets.
7. **Score topical authority.** For each competitor, rough-count the pages they have on each major topic in your space. A competitor with 80 pages on a topic vs your 0 is signalling depth that's hard to displace head-on — a clue to either match their depth or pick a more specific niche.
8. **Synthesise into opportunities.** Translate raw findings into 5-15 prioritised opportunities, scored on impact / effort / fit. This is the artifact that feeds into the [SEO Wins](../05.%20SEO%20Wins/README.md) backlog.

## Common pitfalls

- **Picking competitors lazily.** "Whoever the founder thinks of" is rarely the right SEO competitor set. Always validate via overlap reports.
- **Confusing inspiration with strategy.** Studying a competitor isn't an end — it's a means. The deliverable is *opportunities*, not "here's what they do."
- **Ignoring publishers.** For most D2C brands the largest SEO competitor on commercial terms is a publisher (Wirecutter, NYT, Good Housekeeping). You can't out-resource them, but you can win adjacent angles, long-tail variants, and brand-distinctive content.
- **Copying content, format, and length.** Matching surface features won't earn you the rank. Match the *intent* and *depth signal*; differentiate on angle, expertise, and brand.
- **Doing it once.** Competitor positions shift constantly. Re-run the gap reports quarterly at minimum.
- **Underestimating Reddit and YouTube.** When these rank in your SERPs, they're indicating intent that long-form articles can't fully serve. Take the cue and design content that addresses what those formats reveal users want.
